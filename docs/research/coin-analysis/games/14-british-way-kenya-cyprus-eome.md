# The British Way: Kenya, Cyprus and Arab Revolt (Enemy of My Enemy)

This file has three game sections and one cross-game section. Rules citations use rulebook section numbers. "Playbook" means the Campaign Booklet. "Inferred" marks a design judgment, not a printed rule.

# Part 1. Kenya

## Identity

- Title: The British Way: Kenya. A game in The British Way COIN multipack (1.0). Rules header: "2nd Edition". Page footer: (c) 2026 GMT Games.
- COIN volume: the multipack holds four games (Palestine, Malaya, Kenya, Cyprus) (1.0). The volume number is not printed in the rules file. Not verified.
- Credits: "Designed by Stephen Rangazas" (p.1). Playbook credits: Game Design Stephen Rangazas; COIN Series Design Volko Ruhnke; Game Development Joe Dewhurst; COIN Series Developer Jason Carr; Art Director Rodger B. MacGowan; Graphic Artist Matthew Wallhead; Game Manuals Charlie Kibler (Playbook credits). Joe Dewhurst wrote the Strategy Guide (p.20).
- Setting: Central Province of Kenya, October 1952 to 1956. Focus on rural counterinsurgency and the "Pipeline" of mass resettlement and detention. Urban unrest is not detailed (1.0).
- Scale: 13 spaces. Two players (1.5). Event deck of 21 cards (Setup).
- Scenario: one, "Kenyan Emergency: 1952-1956" (Setup). The Playbook adds Pre-War Options and a linked campaign. Not read in detail.

## Factions

| Faction | Type | Resources | Forces |
|---|---|---|---|
| British (blue) | Government | None. British Operations cost nothing (3.1) | 10 Troops, 15 Police (8 start Out of Play) (1.2, 1.4, Setup) |
| Mau Mau (red) | Insurgent | 0 to 20 (1.8). Start 8 | 15 Guerrillas, 4 Bases (1.2, 1.4) |

- Victory is one shared score: British Political Will (PW), 0 to 20, start 12 (1.10, Setup).
- Early victory at a Propaganda Round check: Mau Mau win if PW is 0, 1 or 2. British win if PW is 18, 19 or 20 (6.1.4, 7.0.1).
- Final victory after the last Propaganda Round: Mau Mau win if PW is below 10. British win if PW is above 10. Exactly 10 is a tie (7.0.2).
- Force notes: Police stand for urban settler police and Home Guard. Bases stand for command, training, supply and administration (1.4 design note). Stacking limit: 2 Bases per space (1.4.2). Guerrillas are Underground or Active (1.4.3).
- If a force type is not Available, a Faction may take it from the map when it places its own forces (1.4.1).

## Sequence of play and eligibility

- One Event card is revealed at a time (2.2). Propaganda cards trigger a Propaganda Round (2.4).
- Two-player Initiative Track, simpler than Colonial Twilight (2.3 note). Mau Mau start 1st Eligible (2.3.1 note).
- Boxes: Limited Operation, Event, Operation and Special Activity, Pass (2.3.2). 2nd Eligible picks from the boxes left.
- Limited Operation: one space, no Special Activity. Deploy, Sweep or March may use many origins but one destination (2.3.4).
- After both act, the cylinder in the left-most box becomes 1st Eligible (2.3.5). Limited Operation gives 1st Eligible next card. Operation and Special Activity gives 2nd Eligible. Only Event or Pass in the Event box blocks the Event (2.3.5 design note).
- Pass: British may shift the Pipeline one step. Mau Mau gain 1 Resource (2.3.3).
- Eligibility order is not changed by the Propaganda Round (2.3.1 note).
- Change from the series core: no Eligible/Ineligible sitting-out; both Factions act on every card.

## Operations and Special Activities

British Operations (3.2). All cost 0. Any number of spaces unless Limited.

| Operation | Effect |
|---|---|
| Deploy (3.2.1) | Place up to 4 cubes in Nairobi and up to 2 Police in each British Controlled Province. Then move any Troops to one non-Mountain Jungle space, even if Limited. |
| Relocate (3.2.2) | In British Controlled Populated Reserves, up to the number of spaces the Pipeline Track shows (not its value; Relocate has its own limit per step): Population -1, one Police Out of Play to Available. Then roll for Outrage. |
| Sweep (3.2.3) | Move adjacent Troops in. Activate 1 Guerrilla per cube (1 per 2 cubes in Mountain Jungle). |
| Assault (3.2.4) | Remove 1 Active enemy piece per Troop (1 per 2 Troops in Mountain Jungle, 1 per cube in Nairobi). Bases only after all Guerrillas. Each Base: +1 PW. |

British Special Activities (4.2):
- Reward (4.2.1): with Deploy or Relocate. One British Controlled Populated space: remove 1 Terror, shift one step to Loyalty.
- Reprisal (4.2.2): with Sweep or Assault. Populated spaces with Troops, up to the number of spaces the Pipeline Track shows (not its value; at value 3 the limit is 2, 6.6.3 note). Set to Neutral, place Terror, move one Guerrilla to an adjacent space. Roll for Outrage.
- Air Strike (4.2.3): any Operation. One Province or Mountain Jungle. Remove 1 Active Guerrilla (2 in Mountain Jungle).

Mau Mau Operations (3.3). Cost 1 Resource per space.

| Operation | Effect |
|---|---|
| Rally (3.3.1) | Any Reserve Province or space without Loyalty. Place 1 Guerrilla or replace 2 with a Base. With a Base: place Guerrillas up to Population plus Bases, or flip all Underground. |
| March (3.3.2) | Move Guerrillas to chosen destinations. They turn Active if the destination is a Populated Non-Reserve or Loyal Reserve and movers plus cubes exceed 3. |
| Terror (3.3.3) | In spaces with an Underground Guerrilla: Activate it, then either place a Terror marker (if none there) and shift once toward Resistance, or remove up to 2 Police. |

There is no Mau Mau Attack. Terror removes Police instead (3.3.3 design note).

Mau Mau Special Activities (4.3):
- Supply (4.3.1): gain 1 Resource per Base in Nairobi plus 1 per other Base linked to Nairobi by spaces with a Guerrilla or Resistance.
- Oath (4.3.2): one Populated Reserve Province with an Underground Guerrilla. Place up to 1 Guerrilla and shift once to Resistance.
- Raid (4.3.3): one Province next to a Mountain Jungle. Either move 1 or 2 Guerrillas out of the jungle and do a free Limited Operation, or move Guerrillas into the jungle and flip them Underground. Costs nothing.

Free Operations and Special Activities from Raid and Events cost no Resources (3.1.2, 5.4).

## Event cards and deck

- 35 cards: 32 Events (K1 to K32) and 3 Propaganda (K33 to K35) (1.2, 9.0).
- Deck build: remove the 3 Propaganda cards. Draw 18 Events at random (3 piles of 6). Put a Propaganda card with 2 Events in each pile and place those 3 under the other 4. The 3 piles of 7 form a 21-card deck. 14 Events are unused (Setup).
- Dual Use: pick the unshaded or the shaded text. Either player may pick either (5.2).
- Capabilities: dual-use cards labeled British or Mau Mau. Labels are flavor. Effect lasts the rest of the game. A Capability marker shows the side chosen (5.3).
- Event overrides rules except stacking, Available-only placement and the 0 to 20 limits (5.1.1).
- Each card has flavor text, a Tips line and a Background note that cites David Anderson's Histories of the Hanged by chapter (9.0).
- Content: Operation Anvil (K1), Kenyatta (K2), Lari Massacre (K4), Home Guard (K6), pseudo-gangs (K7), Hola Detention Camp (K26), Protected Villages (K27), Oathing (K28), Dedan Kimathi (K22), Racist Propaganda (K32) (9.0).
- Pipeline and Outrage cards: K24 Labour Party, K25 McLean Inquiry, K26 Hola, K32 (9.0).

## Map, spaces and tracks

- Spaces (1.3): 9 Provinces, 2 Mountain Jungles (Aberdares, Mount Kenya) and Nairobi. Southern Provinces is decoration only (1.3.1).
- Provinces have Population 1 or 2 (1.3.2). Reserves: Meru, Nyeri, Embu, Fort Hall, Kiambu. Non-Reserves: Thompson's Falls, Rift Valley, Nanyuki, Thika (1.3.2). Relocate lowers Reserve Population. Population 0 is Unpopulated and loses its Loyalty or Resistance marker (1.3.2, 1.6).
- Mountain Jungles have Population 0 and never hold Loyalty or Resistance (1.3.3). Nairobi has Population 2 and counts as Populated Non-Reserve (1.3.4).
- Out of Play box holds Police released by Relocate and Events (1.3.5).
- Loyalty and Resistance: three levels per space (Loyal, Neutral, Resistance). A "shift" removes an opposite marker or places one. "Set" replaces (1.6).
- Control: more own pieces than the enemy. Gaining Mau Mau Control lowers PW by the space Population at once. Losing it raises PW by the same amount (1.7).
- Resources: Mau Mau only, 0 to 20 (1.8).
- Terror markers: one per space from Terror Operation and Reprisal. Events may add more (1.4.2, 1.9).
- PW track 0 to 20 (1.10).
- Pipeline Track: set by British Pass, end of each Propaganda Round, and Events (6.6.1). Named steps seen in text: Emergency Powers (1), Villagization (2), Prison System (3) (Example of Play 8.0). Full range and names are not verified.
- Pipeline sets how many spaces Relocate and Reprisal may use (6.6.2). Use each action's own printed limit from the track, not the track value. Only the Outrage roll compares the die with the track value (6.6.3). The rules do not give a full value table. The Example of Play says Relocate takes 1 space at value 2, 2 at value 4 and none at 0 or 1, and Reprisal takes 2 at value 3 (8.0). This is not fully consistent. Not verified.
- Outrage: roll one die once per Relocate or Reprisal. Below the Pipeline value: PW -1. No roll at 0 or 1 (6.6.3).
- Adjacency: spaces that border (1.3.6).

## Propaganda / Coup / Winter round

Phases in order (6.0):

1. Political Will phase (6.1). Add all changes first, then move PW.
   - Kikuyu Resistance (6.1.1): Loyal Population minus Resistance Population. If negative, lower PW by it. Never raises PW.
   - Settlers Intimidated (6.1.2): -1 per Populated Non-Reserve space with Terror.
   - Mau Mau Contained (6.1.3): +2 if no Mau Mau pieces in Nairobi. +2 if no Bases outside Mountain Jungles.
   - Victory check (6.1.4). Game end check on the last Propaganda card (6.1.5).
2. Resource phase: Mau Mau gain Resources equal to Bases on the map, up to 20 (6.2).
3. Support phase: British may Reward. Mau Mau may Oath (6.3).
4. Redeploy phase: British must move Troops out of Mountain Jungles to British Controlled spaces or Nairobi. Then British may move Police to British Controlled Provinces or Nairobi. Mau Mau may free Rally in each Mountain Jungle ("Forest Camps") (6.4). Then adjust Control.
5. Reset phase: remove all Terror. Flip all Guerrillas Underground. British may shift the Pipeline one step. Reveal the next card (6.5).

## Non-player (bot) system

None in this rulebook. The game is for two human players (1.5).

## Unique mechanics

- Pipeline Track plus Outrage die roll. Harsh British tools carry political risk (6.6).
- Population is a changeable resource. Relocate cuts Reserve Population and frees Police (3.2.2).
- Single score for both sides (PW). Territory, loyalty and Terror all convert to PW (7.1, 7.2).
- Mau Mau cannot attack Troops. They remove Police through Terror (3.3.3).
- Mountain Jungles are a sanctuary. Raid and the free Redeploy Rally support it (4.3.3, 6.4.2).
- Mau Mau have no free resource engine. Supply needs a path to Nairobi (4.3.1).
- Nairobi Deploy ignores British Control (8.0 example).

## Relevance

- Inferred: the Pipeline plus Outrage roll is a small model for repression with a political price. A PKK game could use a similar "repression level" track that gates cordon and village-clearing actions and risks a score loss.
- Inferred: Mountain Jungle sanctuary with Raid and free Redeploy Rally maps to mountain base areas near the Iraq border.
- Inferred: Relocate (Population falls, forces rise) maps to village evacuation and village guard recruitment.
- Inferred: the single-score design (PW) keeps a two-player game short but hides ethnic and civilian detail.

# Part 2. Cyprus

## Identity

- Title: The British Way: Cyprus. A game in The British Way multipack (1.0). Header: "2nd Edition". Footer: (c) 2026 GMT Games.
- Credits: "Designed by Stephen Rangazas" (p.1). Developer and series credits as for Kenya (Playbook credits). Strategy Guide by Joe Dewhurst (p.20). The design note credits Fred Serval's A Gest of Robin Hood for Prison and the Sabotage roll (1.3.6 design note).
- Setting: Greek Cypriot EOKA insurgency, November 1955 to the London-Zurich Agreements of 1959. EOKA wants enosis (union with Greece) (1.0).
- Scale: 10 spaces. Two players. 21-card deck (Setup).
- Scenario: one, "Cyprus Emergency: 1955-1959" (Setup).

## Factions

| Faction | Type | Special resource | Forces |
|---|---|---|---|
| British (blue) | Government | Intel Chits: 9 chits, values 0 to 2, three of each (1.6.1) | 12 Troops, 6 Police (1.2, 1.4) |
| EOKA (red) | Insurgent | Arms Caches (1.6.2) | 12 Cells, 4 Arms Caches (1.2, 1.4) |

- Neither side uses normal Resources (1.6).
- Victory is British Political Will (PW), 0 to 15, start 14 (1.8, Setup).
- EOKA win only if PW is 0 at a Propaganda Round check (6.1.4, 7.0.1). If EOKA have not won by the end of the last Propaganda Round, the British win (6.1.5, 7.0.2).
- Cells replace Guerrillas. Arms Caches replace Bases. Reason: small cells limited by arms (1.4 design note).
- Intel Chits: drawn at random from a bag, public once held. Spend value to remove Underground Cells in Patrol and Assault. A chit's full value must be used in one space. Discard any chit to add a space or to skip Curfew in Search (1.6.1, 3.2.3).
- Arms Caches are "used" to pick Recruit spaces. They are "spent" to boost Sabotage or fund an Offensive. Never in British Bases (1.6.2, 1.4.2).
- Stacking: 2 Arms Caches per space. 1 Curfew per Town. Sabotage markers stack without limit (1.4.2).
- Placement: forces may only come from Available. No taking from the map (1.4.1).

## Sequence of play and eligibility

- Same Initiative Track as Kenya (2.3). EOKA start 1st Eligible and return to 1st Eligible at every Reset (2.3.1 note, 6.4).
- Pass: British draw 1 Intel Chit. EOKA may Ambush in one space (2.3.3).
- Limited Operation: one space. Deploy, Patrol or Search may use many origins and one destination (2.3.4).
- Intelligence Lead and Offensive (2.3.7): British discard one Intel Chit to add one space to Patrol, Search or Assault. EOKA return one Arms Cache (from a space with no British cubes) to Available to add one space to Recruit, Sabotage or Ambush. This works on Limited Operations too.
- Normal Operations use up to 3 spaces (3.1).

## Operations and Special Activities

British Operations (3.2):

| Operation | Effect |
|---|---|
| Deploy (3.2.1) | Up to 3 Town or Base spaces with more Troops than EOKA pieces: place 1 Police each. Then move any Troops from anywhere to one space. |
| Patrol (3.2.2) | Move any Police to up to 3 non-Mountain spaces with Cells. In one space remove 1 Active Cell to Prison per Police. Spend Intel Chits to remove Underground Cells. |
| Search (3.2.3) | Move adjacent Troops in. Mountain or Base: Activate 1 Cell per cube. Town: place Curfew and Activate 1 Cell per 3 cubes, or discard an Intel Chit to Activate 1 per cube with no Curfew. |
| Assault (3.2.4) | Remove 1 Active piece per Troop (1 per 2 in Mountains). Cells first (alternating Available and Prison, Available first), then Arms Caches. Each Arms Cache: +1 PW and draw 1 Intel Chit. Spend Intel Chits for Underground Cells. |

British Special Activities (4.2):
- Restore (4.2.1): with Deploy. One space with Troops, Police and no EOKA: remove Curfew and Sabotage.
- Diplomacy (4.2.2): with Deploy, Patrol or Search. If 3 or fewer Towns hold Sabotage or Curfew, International Opinion goes one step left. Otherwise roll above the track value to do so.
- Interrogate (4.2.3): with Search or Assault. Draw 1 Intel Chit per 2 Cells in Prison.

EOKA Operations (3.3). No Resource cost.

| Operation | Effect |
|---|---|
| Recruit (3.3.1) | Up to 3 Mountain or Town spaces without Curfew. Mountain with Arms Cache: 2 Cells. Mountain without: 1. Town with or next to an Arms Cache: 1. Not in Bases. |
| Travel (3.3.2) | Up to 3 origins without Curfew. Move Cells to any spaces (not only adjacent). They end Underground. They turn Active if the destination is a Base or Curfew space and movers plus cubes exceed 3. |
| Sabotage (3.3.3) | Towns or Bases with an Underground Cell. Activate it. Optional: spend an adjacent or local Arms Cache for +2. Roll a die, -1 per Police. Above 2: place a Sabotage marker. |
| Ambush (3.3.4) | Towns with an Underground Cell. Activate it, remove 1 Police. Roll above remaining cubes: place an Arms Cache in or next to the space. |

EOKA Special Activities (4.3):
- Smuggle (4.3.1): with Recruit or Travel. Place 1 Arms Cache in a Mountain or Town with a Cell and no cubes, or move one from a space with a Cell to an adjacent Mountain or Town with a Cell. Not allowed at Ignored. Two spaces at Condemned.
- Infiltrate (4.3.2): any Operation. One space with Police and at least as many Underground Cells as Police: replace a Police with a Cell. Discard the highest Intel Chit.
- Propagandize (4.3.3): any Operation. One space with a Cell and a Sabotage or Curfew marker. Activate a Cell if all are Underground. Then PW -1 (Sabotage there) or International Opinion one step right (Curfew, no Sabotage).

## Event cards and deck

- 35 cards: C1 to C32 Events and C33 to C35 Propaganda (9.0). Same 21-card deck build as Kenya (Setup).
- Intel Chits go in a cup (Setup).
- Dual Use as in Kenya (5.2). Capabilities labeled British or EOKA. One "Dual Capability", C31 Turkish Police, helps and harms both sides (5.3).
- Event stacking limits and the PW limits 0 to 15 apply (5.1.1).
- Content: Makarios (C1), Grivas (C2), AKEL (C3), Suez Crisis (C4), Turkish Government (C5), Operation Pepperpot (C6), Volkan/TMT (C26), Greek Government (C22), Macmillan Plan (C29), Radio Athens (C14), Special Branch (C17). Backgrounds cite David French, Fighting EOKA, by chapter (9.0).
- Suez Crisis removes 3 Troops until the next Reset (C4).

## Map, spaces and tracks

- Spaces (1.3.1): 6 Towns, 2 Mountains (Troodos, Kyrenia), 2 British Bases (Akrotiri, Dhekelia).
- Towns carry a Sabotage value of 1 or 2 (Nicosia 2). Mountains 0. Bases 2 (1.3.2 to 1.3.4, 6.1.2).
- Karpas is split from Famagusta for size (1.3.2 design note).
- Prison box: Cells removed by Patrol, Assault and some Events. Half return at Reset, rounded down (1.3.6).
- Curfew markers: pool of 4, Towns only. They block Recruit and Travel and help Search and Propagandize. A Curfew leaves when its space has no British pieces, by Restore, or at Reset (1.10).
- International Opinion Track, range 0 to 4. Named steps in text: Ignored (0), Debated (2), Condemned (4). The Playbook lists Criticized as 3 (Playbook 5.x). The Example of Play calls step 1 "Discussed" and then moves "from 1 to 2" as Discussed to Criticized (8.0). Names are not fully verified.
- PW track 0 to 15 (1.8). No Control, Support or Opposition in this game (Playbook designer notes).
- Adjacency by border (1.3.5).

## Propaganda / Coup / Winter round

1. Political Will phase (6.1). Total all changes first.
   - International Pressure (6.1.1): shift International Opinion right once per 3 Cells in Prison. Then lower PW by the track value (0 to 4).
   - Disorder (6.1.2): -1 or -2 per Sabotage marker by space value.
   - Order (6.1.3): +2 if no Arms Caches in Mountains. +1 per 2 Towns without Sabotage.
   - Victory check at PW 0 (6.1.4). Game end on the final card (6.1.5).
2. Resource phase (6.2): EOKA place 1 Arms Cache in a Town or Mountain with a Cell. British with no Intel Chits draw 1. Otherwise British discard half (round down).
3. Redeploy phase (6.3): British may move Police among spaces with British pieces. British must move Troops out of Mountains to either Base. EOKA must move Cells out of Bases to Towns with EOKA pieces or Mountains. EOKA may move 1 Arms Cache to a space with a Cell.
4. Reset phase (6.4): if 3 or fewer Towns hold Sabotage or Curfew, International Opinion steps toward Ignored. Remove all Curfew and Sabotage. Return half the Prison Cells. Flip Cells Underground. Set EOKA 1st Eligible.

## Non-player (bot) system

None in this rulebook (1.5).

## Unique mechanics

- Intel Chit bag with random values. Intelligence is the British bottleneck (1.6.1).
- Prison as a loss pool with partial return. Prison size feeds International Opinion (1.3.6, 6.1.1).
- Sabotage as a die roll with Police penalty and Arms Cache boost (3.3.3).
- Curfew as a double-edged tool. It stops Recruit and Travel but gives EOKA a Propagandize target (1.10, 4.3.3).
- Travel selects origins and moves to any space (3.3.2 note).
- Infiltrate strips Police and Intel (4.3.2).
- No taking pieces from the map (1.4.1 design note).
- PW has no instant British win (7.0).

## Relevance

- Inferred: the Intel Chit plus Prison loop models the real bottleneck of an urban or mixed campaign. A PKK game could use a similar "intelligence" token that turns detentions into new targets.
- Inferred: International Opinion with Greece and Turkey as external actors maps to Turkey, Syria, Iraq, Iran, the EU and Kurdish diaspora pressure.
- Inferred: Cells and Arms Caches fit the early PKK years (arms limited, small groups) better than Guerrillas and Bases.
- Inferred: EOKA's early victory by PW 0 only, with a British default win, makes a clean "survive to the end" asymmetry.

# Part 3. Arab Revolt (Enemy of My Enemy)

## Identity

- Title: The British Way: Arab Revolt. A game in the Enemy of My Enemy (EOME) expansion for The British Way. Its companion is Japanese Occupation (1.0). No edition line is printed in this rules file. Not verified.
- Credits: "Designed by Stephen Rangazas" (p.1). No other credits in this file. Not verified for EOME.
- Setting: Palestinian Arab rebels against British rule, 1936 to 1939. Focus from the 1937 rural revolt to the 1939 White Paper (1.0). The game models internal splits on both sides, such as Jewish armed groups aiding the British (1.0).
- Standalone game. It uses the Palestine board plus two overlay tiles: Rebel Available Forces and Unity Track (1.2, 1.3).
- Scale: 6 Districts, 3 Cities, 4 Railways, 4 Foreign Country spaces (1.3). Two players. 21-card deck (Setup).
- Scenario: one, "Arab Revolt: 1937-1939" (Setup).

## Factions

| Faction | Type | Special resource | Forces |
|---|---|---|---|
| British (blue) | Government. Includes Jewish paramilitaries and Arab peace bands (1.5 design note) | Punishment chits (1.7) | 15 Troops, 9 Police, 6 Irregulars (1.2, 1.4) |
| Rebels (red) | Insurgent. AHC, local commanders, foreign fighters (1.5 design note) | Operations chits (1.8) | 15 Guerrillas, 5 Bases (1.2, 1.4) |

- Victory is British PW, 0 to 20, start 18 (1.10, Setup).
- Rebels win at a Propaganda Round check if PW is 0 (6.1.5, 7.0.1). British win at the end of the last Propaganda Round if Rebels have not won (6.1.6, 7.0.2).
- Irregulars: British pieces that can be Underground or Active. They can Activate for Divide. Rebel Ambush removes them (1.4.3, 3.3.4, 4.2.2).
- Punishment chits: 6 chits, 2 each of Detain, Demolish, Fine. Each has a Punishment effect and a Mobilization effect for the Rebels (1.7).
  - Detain: remove 1 Guerrilla to Prison, then place Opposition (Mobilization).
  - Demolish: remove Opposition, then place 1 Guerrilla (Mobilization).
  - Fine: remove 1 Sabotage from an adjacent Railway. No Mobilization.
- Operations chits: 9 chits, values 1 to 3, three each. Rebels draw 2 each round (1 at Decentralized) and pick one before choosing a box (1.8, 2.3.4). Value sets how many spaces Rebel Operations may use, even for Limited Operations (1.8.2).
- Chit effect, resolved by the British after the Rebel action, even on Event or Pass: value 3, replace 1 Active Guerrilla with an Irregular. Value 2, remove 1 Active Guerrilla. Value 1, no effect (1.8.2). Ignored at Centralized (6.6.3).
- Placement from the map is allowed when a type is not Available. Prison pieces cannot be moved this way (1.4.1).
- Stacking: 2 Rebel Bases per space. Bases never on Railways. No British pieces in Foreign Countries. 1 Curfew per District or City (1.4.2).

## Sequence of play and eligibility

- Same Initiative Track as Kenya, with Rebels first (2.3, 2.3.1 note).
- Rebels draw and choose an Operations chit before they pick a box, even to play the Event or Pass (2.3.4).
- Limited Operation: for the British, one space. For Rebels, spaces up to chit value, no Special Activity (2.3.5).
- Pass: British select one Punishment chit to hold. Rebels may place a Base in a District or Foreign Country with a Guerrilla (2.3.3). Ignore the Pass text printed on the track (2.3.3 note).
- Mandatory Events (5.4): A11 Italian Aid and A24 Tensions in Europe. They fire when drawn, before the 1st Eligible acts. Nobody may take the Event box that round.
- Curfews are removed at Reset (2.3.1 bullet, 6.5).

## Operations and Special Activities

British Operations (3.2). Up to 3 spaces (3.1).

| Operation | Effect |
|---|---|
| Deploy (3.2.1) | Districts or Cities with more British than Rebel pieces. Place up to 2 Police or 1 Irregular per space. In one space, place a Curfew, or remove an existing Curfew and Opposition. |
| Patrol (3.2.2) | Move any cubes to up to 3 Cities and Railways. On Railways Activate 1 Guerrilla per cube. Optional free Assault on one Railway. |
| Sweep (3.2.3) | Move adjacent Troops in. Activate 1 Guerrilla per 2 cubes, or 1 per cube if the British Activate an Underground Irregular there. |
| Assault (3.2.4) | Neutral District: 1 piece per Troop. Neutral City or Railway: 1 per cube. Opposition District: 1 per 2 Troops. Opposition City: 1 per 2 cubes. Bases last. Each Base removed shifts Unity toward Decentralized. |

British Special Activities (4.2):
- Punish (4.2.1): any Operation. Draw 2 Punishment chits, pick one. Apply its Punishment effect in 2 Districts or Cities with Troops, then its Mobilization effect in the same spaces.
- Divide (4.2.2): with Deploy or Patrol. Up to Unity-value spaces: Activate an Underground Irregular to replace a Guerrilla with an Irregular (in or next to the space). Or roll: at or below the Irregulars on the map, shift Unity toward Decentralized.
- Air Recon (4.2.3): any Operation. Up to 2 Districts or Railways: Activate 1 Guerrilla each.

Rebel Operations (3.3). No Resource cost. Spaces limited by chit value.

| Operation | Effect |
|---|---|
| Rally (3.3.1) | Non-Railway spaces without Curfew that hold Rebel pieces or Opposition. Place 1 Guerrilla (2 with a Base). In one District with a Base and more Rebels than British, add Opposition. |
| March (3.3.2) | Select origins. Move Guerrillas to adjacent spaces. They turn Active if the destination is a Railway or Curfew space and movers plus British forces exceed 3. They do not go Underground. |
| Sabotage (3.3.3) | Spaces with an Underground Guerrilla. Activate it. Place Sabotage on a Railway, or Opposition on a District or City. |
| Ambush (3.3.4) | Spaces with an Underground Guerrilla and Police or Irregulars. Activate it, remove 1 such piece. If 3 or more removed in total, PW -1. |

Rebel Special Activities (4.3):
- Mobilize (4.3.1): with Rally or March. Up to 2 Districts (more Guerrillas than Irregulars) or Foreign Countries: place a Base. Then place a Guerrilla in each Opposition space.
- Coordinate (4.3.2): any Operation. +1 to the chit value. Roll at or below Rebel Bases: shift Unity toward Centralized.
- Raid (4.3.3): with Rally, Sabotage or Ambush. Up to 2 spaces next to a Rebel Base: move in 1 or 2 Guerrillas from the adjacent Base space. Optional Limited Sabotage or Ambush there.

British Operations and Special Activities may never select Foreign Countries. Rebels may use them for Rally, March and Mobilize (1.3.7).

- 35 cards: A1 to A32 Events and A33 to A35 Propaganda (9.0). Deck build as in Kenya, but the 3 Propaganda cards are shuffled at random into the piles (Setup).

## Map, spaces and tracks

- Spaces (1.3.1): Districts (6, rural), Cities (Haifa, Tel Aviv-Jaffa, Jerusalem), Railways (4), Foreign Countries (Lebanon, Syria, Transjordan, Egypt).
- Lebanon and Syria start in play. Transjordan and Egypt start with Out of Play markers and join through Events (1.3.7, A30).
- Ignore the printed Population or Sabotage numbers in Districts and Cities (1.3.3 note).
- Railways are separate spaces next to every space they touch. Sabotage on them does not block movement but counts for victory. No Bases there (1.3.4).
- Opposition: two levels only, Opposition or Neutral. The British cannot build Support. The design note says British rule had little real support (1.6 design note).
- Prison: Rebel pieces (Guerrillas, sometimes Bases) go there. All return at Reset (1.3.6, 6.5).
- Unity Track (overlay on the Haganah Track): starts at 1, the centre space (Setup). It sets Divide spaces and has two ends (6.6):
  - Centralized (far right): Rebels ignore chit effects (6.6.3).
  - Decentralized (far left): Rebels draw only 1 chit. Each further shift toward Decentralized gives the British +1 PW (6.6.4).
  - Number of steps is not verified. The Example of Play suggests value 0 at Centralized (8.0).
- Curfew pool of 3 markers (1.12). Out of Play box holds 5 Troops at start (Setup).
- PW track 0 to 20 (1.10).

## Propaganda / Coup / Winter round

1. Political Will phase (6.1). Total all changes first.
   - Sabotage (6.1.1): place Sabotage on each Railway with more Guerrillas than British pieces. Then -1 per Sabotaged Railway and +1 per 2 clean Railways.
   - Rebellion Contained (6.1.2): -2 per City or District with more Rebel than British pieces. +1 per 2 Cities or Districts with no Rebel pieces or Opposition.
   - Rebel Leaders (6.1.3): -1 per District with a Rebel Base.
   - Enemy of My Enemy (6.1.4): -1 per 3 Irregulars on the map.
   - Victory (6.1.5). Game end (6.1.6).
2. Resources phase (6.2): British pick one Punishment chit to hold and return the rest. Rebels roll: at or below Bases, they either shift Unity toward Centralized or hold one Operations chit. Then Foreign Assistance: draw an Operations chit and Rally in Foreign Countries up to its value.
3. Support phase (6.3): British may Divide. Rebels may Mobilize.
4. Redeploy phase (6.4): British must move Troops on Railways to Cities. They may move Police to Railways or Cities and may remove up to half the Irregulars. Rebels must move Guerrillas on Railways and in Cities to spaces with Rebel Bases, or else to Foreign Countries.
5. Reset phase (6.5): remove Curfew and Sabotage. Return Prison pieces. Flip all Guerrillas and Irregulars Underground. A34 adds its Troop return.

## Non-player (bot) system

None in this rulebook (1.5).

## Unique mechanics

- Rebel Operations chits: random capacity per turn, a decentralization cost for the insurgent (1.8, design note).
- Punishment chits with a built-in backlash (Mobilization) for the British (1.7).
- Irregulars as British pieces whose number lowers PW (6.1.4).
- Unity Track links to Bases, chits and Divide (6.6).
- Foreign Country spaces as sanctuaries that Rebels staff through Foreign Assistance (6.2.3).
- Mandatory Events for external shocks (5.4).
- Opposition only, no Support (1.6).

## Relevance

- Inferred: Operations chits are a direct model of a decentralized insurgency. A PKK game for the 1984 start could use a low-capacity random chit for early weak command and a higher fixed value after centralization.
- Inferred: Foreign Country spaces with Foreign Assistance fit PKK sanctuary in Syria (Bekaa Valley), Iraq and Iran. British exclusion from Foreign Countries maps to cross-border limits on the Turkish state.
- Inferred: Irregulars with a PW cost fit village guards, who help the state but cost it legitimacy.
- Inferred: Mandatory Events model external shocks, such as coups or Cold War shifts, that no player controls.

# Part 4. Cross-game notes

## Rules shared with Palestine and Malaya (compared by headings and grep only)

I did not read the Palestine or Malaya rules in full. Counts come from grep of the rule files.

- All five rule files share the same skeleton: 1.0 to 9.0, same Initiative Track text, same Event rules 5.1 to 5.3 and the same Propaganda Round name. PW track and Propaganda Round are common.
- Kenya and Malaya: Rally, March and Terror; Sweep and Assault; Reprisal and Air Strike appear in Malaya (grep: Reprisal 16, Air Strike 6 in Malaya). Both use Resources, Control and Support or Opposition (by heading). Malaya has Commander and New Villages tracks; Kenya has Pipeline and Outrage. "Outrage" appears in Kenya (15) and once in Palestine, and not in Malaya. Pipeline is Kenya only. Cyprus text notes that Kenya and Malaya also use March.
- Cyprus and Palestine: Cells, Arms Caches, Intel Chits, Prison, Curfew, Deploy, Patrol, Search, Assault, Restore, Recruit, Travel, Sabotage, Propagandize and the 2.3.7 Intelligence Lead and Offensive rule (grep: Intel Chit 36 in Palestine and 32 in Cyprus; Arms Cache 59 and 58). Differences by heading: Palestine has Rob, Silence, Terror, Negotiate, Mass Detention, Haganah Track and Starred Events (5.4). Cyprus has Ambush, Smuggle, Infiltrate, Diplomacy, Interrogate and International Opinion.
- Arab Revolt and Palestine: same board, Railways, Cities, Prison, Curfew, Deploy, Patrol, Sweep (Palestine uses Search), Assault, Sabotage. Arab Revolt replaces the Haganah Track with the Unity Track (1.2). It uses Guerrillas and Bases like Malaya and Kenya (1.4 design note).
- Arab Revolt is unique in using Operations chits, Punishment chits, Irregulars and Mandatory Events (grep: Operations chit only in Arab Revolt).
- Kenya and Malaya share 5.4 "Free Operations". Palestine has 5.4 "Starred Events". Cyprus has no 5.4.
- Playbook: Kenya and Malaya form one family; Cyprus and Palestine another (Playbook 3.x).

## Counterinsurgency design lessons for a Turkey/PKK game (inferred)

1. Inferred: give the state a political cost for repression. Kenya's Pipeline and Outrage, Arab Revolt's Mobilization and Cyprus's Prison to International Opinion all show the same idea. The harsh tool is strong but feeds the insurgent.
2. Inferred: model the insurgent's real limit, not Resources. Arms (Cyprus), command unity (Arab Revolt) and low supply (Kenya) are the three limits here. For the early PKK, arms and cross-border sanctuary are better choices than money.
3. Inferred: use one shared score (PW) for a two-player game. It cuts piece count. A PKK game would need to add a second score or a civilian layer to show Kurdish population attitudes and state control of the southeast.
4. Inferred: use Mandatory or random external events for outside actors (Syria, Iraq, USSR, EU). Arab Revolt and Cyprus both do this.
5. Inferred: village guards fit the Irregulars and Home Guard pattern. Keep a cost for co-opting local militia.
6. Inferred: the Initiative Track here removes sitting out a card. It suits a 2-player Falling Sky port less than the series core, but it can reduce complexity.
7. Inferred: for a digital Falling Sky, the chit-drawing and bag mechanics (Intel, Punishment, Operations) are easy to code. The open-information rule for held chits reduces hidden-state work.
