# Fall of Saigon (expansion to Fire in the Lake): deltas from the base game

Convention: "Base game" means *Fire in the Lake* (FitL). Rule numbers with an "S" prefix are Fall of Saigon (FoS) rules. Plain numbers are FitL rules that FoS keeps. Base-game rule text is not in the files read. Statements about the base game come only from what the FoS text says about it.

## Identity

- Title: *Fall of Saigon*. A sequel expansion. A copy of FitL is required (S1.0).
- Series: COIN Series, Volume IV (rulebook cover).
- Rules authors as printed: Mark Herman and Volko Ruhnke (rulebook cover).
- Playbook credits: Game Design Mark Herman and Volko Ruhnke. COIN Series Development Jason Carr. Additional Development Joe Dewhurst and Stephen Rangazas. Non-player (Jacquard) design Bruce Mansfield. Non-player development Kevin Crooks (Playbook p.36).
- Edition: (c) 2022 GMT Games (rulebook footer). No edition number is printed. Not verified.
- Setting: South Vietnam, 1973 to 1975. From the Paris Peace Talks to the NVA conquest (S1.0).
- Scale and players: same as base game, 1 to 4 players (S1.5). New 2-player scenario Black April: NVA vs ARVN (S1.5).
- Components added (S1.2): 79 cards (Event, Pivotal Event, Coup), 2 extra Ranger pieces, 3 ARVN Bases, 8 NVA and 8 ARVN Armor wedges, US Posture tile, 2-player Initiative tile, 24 Captured and Destroyed markers (S1.4.5), 24 Non-player cards (Tây Sơn bot).
- Rule numbering: each FoS rule uses the base number with "S" in front. Unchanged base rules are skipped (S1.0).
- Scenarios (S2.1):

| Scenario | Players | Start | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Extended Short 1972-1975 "Tunnel's End" | 1 to 4 | 1972 | Own setup. Paris Talks come after the initial deck. |
| Extended Medium 1968-1975 "Nixon's War" | 1 to 4 | 1968 | FitL "A Better War" setup plus FoS changes. |
| Extended Full 1964-1975 "Quagmire" | 1 to 4 | 1964 | FitL "Nam" Full setup plus FoS changes. |
| Black April 1973-1975 "Fight for the remaining land" | 2 | 1973 | NVA and ARVN only. US Retreat already happened. |

- Optional rule: in Medium and Full, Factions cannot win before the Coup Rounds after the Paris Talks, if all players agree (S2.1 scenario text).

## Factions

Same four Factions as base game: US, ARVN, NVA, VC (S1.5). Same as base game unless listed.

| Faction | Type | Victory formula (threshold) | FoS change |
|---|---|---|---|
| US | Government | Support + US Available - Anti-War, over 50 | Anti-War subtracts (S7.2). |
| ARVN | Government | COIN Control + Patronage, over 50 | None to formula. COIN Control of Saigon needed in two Victory Phases (S7.2). |
| NVA | Insurgent | NVA Control + NVA Bases - War Weary, over 18 | War Weary subtracts (S7.2). NVA Control of Saigon needed in two Victory Phases. |
| VC | Insurgent | Opposition + VC Bases, over 35 | None. Control of Saigon has no new effect on VC (S7.2). |

Thresholds are the FitL values. They are shown as margins in the New Terms Index: US -50, ARVN -50, NVA -18, VC -35.

- Anti-War Movement: new marker. Starts at 15 at the Paris Talks (S6.8.1). Never below 0 (S7.2, S5.1.1).
- War Weariness: new marker. Starts at 0 at the Paris Talks (S6.8.1). Never below 0.
- Saigon rule: in the Victory Phase of the 1973 or 1974 Coup card (Watergate, Nixon Resigns), a Faction must exceed its threshold and hold Saigon (NVA Control for NVA, COIN Control for US or ARVN) to win (S7.2).
- Resources: VC Operations cost 2 per space after the Talks (S3.3). Playbook says VC are starved of Resources (Playbook p.8, inferred reading of the cost rule).
- Econ: after US Retreat the Econ marker is set aside. It no longer limits US use of ARVN Resources (S1.8.1, S6.8.2).
- Black April: no US or VC Faction. Set the US and VC victory markers aside (S7.2). Set aside Econ, Support+Avail and Oppose+Bases markers (S2.1 Black April).
- New pieces (S1.4): 2 ARVN Rangers, 3 ARVN Bases, 8 ARVN Armor, 8 NVA Armor. All enter at the Paris Talks (S6.8.3).
- Armor is a new force type for ARVN and NVA. It acts like Troops in most moves, and hits harder (see Operations).
- Captured Armor (S1.4.5): pool of 16 Captured and 8 Destroyed markers in an opaque cup. Spearhead draws one at random. Captured: the piece works for the capturing side. Destroyed: the Armor goes Out of Play. Re-capture succeeds with no draw. Other removal sends Captured Armor Out of Play.

## Sequence of play and eligibility

- Extended scenarios: same as base game 4-player sequence (S2.3). New Pivotal Events replace the FitL ones at the Talks (S2.3.8, S6.8.3).
- New Pivotal rules: NVA Spring Offensive may be played with a Coup card showing. US Accords Enforced may be played once per Campaign (S2.3.8). The Playbook adds that it returns to the US after each Coup Round and needs COIN Control under 18 (Playbook p.3).
- Black April uses the 2-player sequence from *Colonial Twilight* (S2.3 2-player note). Rules:
  - Only the current Event card is visible. No look-ahead card.
  - One player is First Eligible, one is Second Eligible. Ignore the Faction symbols and "stay Eligible" or "Ineligible" text (S2.3.1, S5.1.1).
  - First Eligible may play the Event, do an Operation with or without a Special Activity, or Pass (S2.3.2).
  - Second Eligible picks a box next to the First Eligible cylinder: Event, Limited Op, Op Only, Op and Special Activity, or Pass.
  - Pass gives +1 Resource to NVA or +3 to ARVN. Both may Pass and Eligibility stays (S2.3.3).
  - After an Op with Special Activity, or an Op in several spaces, the Second Eligible becomes First Eligible. Anything else leaves the order unchanged (S2.3.5).
  - Pivotal Event: playable before the First Eligible chooses. The Second Eligible wins a conflict. The user of the Pivotal becomes First Eligible. The other player may only Op with Special Activity or Pass (S2.3.7).
  - No Monsoon in the 2-player sequence (S2.3.7 note).
- Coup cards: the three FoS Coup cards are not RVN Leaders (S2.4.1). The last one, Saigon Stands Alone, ends the game at once with no Coup Round (S2.4.2).
- Names of the Coup cards: Watergate (1973), Nixon Resigns (1974), Saigon Stands Alone (1975) (S7.2; Playbook p.3).
- Unilateral US Retreat: the US may Retreat in the 1973 or 1974 Coup Round if still at War (S6.1).

## Operations and Special Activities

Same as base game except as listed.

**US (Extended, after US Retreat; S3.2, S4.2, S6.9.3)**
- Only Train and Advise. No Patrol, Sweep or Assault. At Posture 0 only Events or Pass.
- Train may also place 1 ARVN Armor instead of cubes. Post-Paris, Train works at ARVN Bases with no US piece (S3.2.1).
- Advise as an Operation (new, S3.2.5): 0 Resources, no added Special Activity, up to Posture spaces, adds Aid equal to Posture.
- Special Activities: Air Strike or Air Lift only at Posture 4. Otherwise only Advise (S4.2). Air Lift cannot move Armor.
- Advise cannot remove NVA Armor with Special Forces. Post-Paris, Advise may instead move any US Irregulars on the map to the Advise spaces and flip them Underground (S4.2.1).
- Air Strike removes NVA Armor only after all NVA Troops (S4.2.3).

**ARVN**
- Train: may place Armor instead of cubes. After US Retreat, Armor placed per Train is capped by Posture (S3.2.1, S6.9.2). After US Retreat, a Training Operation may instead of Pacifying, placing a Base or transferring Patronage pay 1 ARVN Resource per piece to move ARVN Casualties to Available, even if the Train was free. It is an alternative to those end options, not an addition (S3.1.2, S3.2.1).
- Patrol and Sweep: Armor moves like Troops. Armor Activates no Guerrillas in Sweep (S3.2.2, S3.2.3).
- Assault: each Armor removes 1 NVA Armor or 2 other enemy pieces. NVA Armor goes after NVA Troops and before Guerrillas and Bases. Post-Paris, Active Rangers remove like Troops (S3.2.4).
- Raid: cannot remove NVA Armor. Post-Paris, Raid may Activate US Irregulars to act as Rangers (S4.3.3).
- Spearhead (new Special Activity, S4.3.4): accompanies Train or Patrol. From up to 2 spaces with Armor, pick up to 2 Armor and up to 2 Troops per Armor. Move them onto Highway LoCs with no enemy, then into adjacent spaces. Not on the two Mekong-only LoCs next to Can Tho. Each Armor moves once. Then Assault for 0 Resources with those units. Enemy Armor that would be removed is instead a Capture attempt: draw one marker from the pool of 16 Captured and 8 Destroyed. Destroyed sends the Armor Out of Play. Captured transfers it to the attacker. Re-Capture of already Captured Armor succeeds automatically (S1.4.5).
- Air Lift and Air Strike (new for ARVN after US Retreat, S4.3.5): with any ARVN Operation. Air Lift moves ARVN Troops, Rangers or US Irregulars up to Posture (no Armor). Air Strike gives hits equal to Posture, no die roll.

**NVA**
- Rally: in North Vietnam only, may place 1 Available Armor instead of other pieces (S3.3.1).
- March and Terror: Armor acts as Troops (S3.3.2, S3.3.4). ARVN Armor does not help Activate Guerrillas (note to S3.3.2).
- Attack: each Armor removes 1 ARVN Armor or 2 other enemy pieces, after Troops or Guerrillas attack. ARVN Armor goes only after all enemy Troops. After US Retreat, removed ARVN pieces go to Casualties (S3.3.3).
- Infiltrate: Trail at 3 allows 1 Armor at one selected space with an NVA Base. Trail at 4 allows 1 Armor at each selected space with an NVA Base (S4.4.1).
- Bombard: may target 1 City (not Saigon) instead of 2 spaces. Remove 1 US or ARVN Troop per 3 NVA Troops in the City and adjacent spaces. The City needs 3 or more COIN Troops or any COIN Base. Cannot remove Armor (S4.4.2).
- Spearhead (S4.4.4): as ARVN Spearhead, with Rally, March or Terror, and a free Attack.

**VC**
- All VC Operations cost 2 Resources per space after the Talks. March and Terror on LoCs stay at 0 (S3.3).
- Subvert: removed ARVN pieces go to Casualties after US Retreat (S4.5.2).

**Event and Operation rules (S5.1.1)**
- After US Retreat, ARVN pieces removed by Events go to Casualties. Events cannot place US Troops or Bases.
- Black April: Events cannot touch US or VC pieces or add VC Resources.

## Event cards and deck

- FoS deck: 79 cards, Event, Pivotal and Coup (S1.2). Event cards are split into 1973, 1974 and 1975 sets of 24 (S2.1).
- Mini-deck build (S2.1 setup): in order VC, ARVN, NVA, then US, each Faction picks one card openly from each year. Then deal 4 more face down per year (6 more in Black April). Shuffle the 8 cards of each year. Shuffle that year's Coup card among the bottom 4. Stack 1973 on 1974 on 1975. In Extended play, this stack goes under the remaining FitL deck after the Talks (S6.8.3).
- Inferred: each year then has 9 cards (8 Events and 1 Coup).
- Period Events option is always used for the 1973-1975 portion (S2.1 note).
- Pivotal Events: at the Talks every Faction discards its unplayed FitL Pivotal and takes its FoS one (S6.8.3). Names from the Playbook: Accords Enforced (#S76), Spring Offensive (#S77), Retrenchment (#S78), Popular Mobilization (#S79) (Playbook p.3). Spring Offensive is NVA and Accords Enforced is US (S2.3.8). The Faction of the other two is not stated in the files read. Inferred: ARVN and VC. Most are playable only after Watergate (S6.8.3 note).
- Capabilities: M-48 and LGBs counters updated for 2nd Edition FitL cards (S5.3).
- Setup reference cards: the Capability cards AAA, Long Range Guns, SA-2s (shaded) and Arc Light, Armored Cavalry, PT-76 (unshaded) start in effect in Extended Short and Black April (S2.1).
- Historical content: Event text covers Resolution 21, Secret Bombing, Four No's, Mekong Offensive, Le Duan, F-5Es and others (Playbook p.3, p.7 and card list). Playbook design notes cover US Congress cuts and Hanoi's Highlands offensive (Playbook p.32-33).
- Playbook also holds Armor strategy, Black April strategy and Card Selection Strategy sections (Playbook contents p.1). Not read in detail.

## Map, spaces and tracks

Same map as base game. Same space types and Control rules. Same as base game except as listed.

- New track: US Posture, 5 levels (0 to 4), akin to the Trail. Events control normal movement (S6.9.1). Reset applies an automatic drift (S6.6). It is placed over the Overflow B box or beside the ARVN player (S2.1).
- Posture starts at 2 (Limited Backing) on US Retreat and in Black April (S6.8.2, S2.1).
- Posture changes: moving from lower to 4 adds +2 Anti-War (S6.9.1). In Reset, 0 goes to 1 and 4 goes to 3 (S6.6).
- Posture effect on ARVN: Train limited to placing Armor in Posture spaces. Air Lift units and Air Strike hits equal Posture (S6.9.2).
- Posture effect on US: see Operations (S6.9.3).
- New victory markers: "NVA+Bases-WarWeary" and "Support+Avail-AntiWar" (S1.9, S7.2).
- Anti-War and War Weary use the edge track (S1.9).
- ARVN Casualties box in use after US Retreat (S6.8.2). It does not count for Aid. Only US Casualties count (S6.2.5).
- 2-player initiative tile covers the Sequence of Play Eligibility boxes (S2.1).
- The map has Highway LoCs and two Mekong-only LoCs by Can Tho that Spearhead cannot use (S4.3.4).
- Starting values: Extended Short has Aid 20, Patronage 25, Trail 3, Econ 15, Resources VC 6 NVA 15 ARVN 30. Black April has Aid 30, Patronage 20, Trail 3, Posture 2, War Weary 15, Resources NVA 15 ARVN 30 (S2.1).
- Out of play: in Black April, 40 US Troops, 6 US Bases, 6 Irregulars, 9 VC Bases and 30 VC Guerrillas are removed (S2.1).

## Propaganda / Coup / Winter round

Base game Coup Round order is kept: Victory, Resources, Support, Redeploy, Commitment, Reset (S6.1 to S6.6). Deltas:

1. Victory (S6.1, S7.2): use the new formulas and the Saigon rule. In Extended scenarios, the Victory Phase of the last FitL Coup card with no winner starts the Paris Peace Talks. Then go to Resources.
2. Resources (S6.2): after US Retreat and in Black April, ARVN earns as usual but Econ is not used (S6.2.3). Ignore VC earnings in Black April (S6.2.4). Count only US Casualties for Aid (S6.2.5).
3. Support (S6.3): in Black April, NVA may Agitate as if VC in up to 4 spaces with NVA pieces and no COIN Control (S6.3.2).
4. Redeploy (S6.4): Armor redeploys as Troops (S6.4.2, S6.4.3). Step 6.4.5 (game end check) is dropped because the last Coup ends the game (S6.4.5).
5. Commitment (S6.5): after US Retreat and in Black April, no US Troops stay on the map (no 10-Troop limit) and no US Bases come out of Available.
6. Reset (S6.6): ARVN (not US) may pay 1 per piece to move Casualties to Available. Posture 0 to 1 and 4 to 3. In 2-player play, skip "All Factions Eligible" and the new top-card reveal.

**Paris Peace Talks (S6.8, new)**
- Step 1, Post-Paris Victory: swap in new victory markers. War Weary 0. Anti-War 15. Support + Available marker moves -15. Put set-aside Armor, Rangers and Bases Out of Play.
- Step 2, War or Peace: compare the NVA score to red ranges and the US score to green ranges on the Paris Peace table.
- Step 3, Choice: War or Peace changes War Weary or Anti-War as the table says. At "Choice", the Faction picks. If both choose, NVA picks first.
- Step 4, Retreat or Failed Talks: cross-reference the table.
- From the Playbook example: NVA score above 15 forces War (+5 War Weary). 12 to 15 gives a choice. Below 12 forces Peace. US score above 32 forces War. 27 to 32 gives a choice. Below 27 forces Retreat. US Retreat lowered Anti-War by 3 in the example (Playbook p.3). The full table is not in the files read. Not verified.
- NVA Retreat: remove NVA Bases in the South to Available. Move other NVA pieces out of the South. VC loses 1 Base from the South. Patronage +5 (S6.8.1 step 4).
- US Retreat: swap US Bases for ARVN Bases. No more US Troops or Bases on the map. Trail +2. Posture set to 2. Econ removed. At Commitment, remove US Troop and Base Casualties Out of Play and move all US Troops to Available. If both sides Retreat, normal Casualty removal and NVA moves its Bases out (S6.8.1).
- After US Retreat (S6.8.2): removed ARVN go to Casualties. No US Troops or Bases enter. Econ ignored. Posture governs.
- End of Talks (S6.8.3): add 8 NVA Armor, 8 ARVN Armor, 2 Rangers and remaining ARVN Bases to Available. Add the FoS mini-deck. Swap Pivotal Events.
- Post-Paris, any outcome (S6.8.4): US Train at ARVN Bases. ARVN Assault uses Active Rangers. VC Ops cost 2. US Advise moves Irregulars. ARVN Raid uses Irregulars. Single-City Bombard. War Weary and Anti-War count in victory.
- Final Coup, Saigon Stands Alone: ends the game. If COIN controls Saigon, Patronage +5 first (New Terms Index). Highest victory margin wins (S7.3, New Terms Index).

## Non-player (bot) system

Tây Sơn, by Bruce Mansfield. Replaces base game section 8.0 for FoS games (S8.1). It is a card-based "Jacquard" system, the third of its kind, after Arjuna and Trưng (Non-player Design Notes).

- Cards: 6 per NP Faction (US at War, ARVN, NVA, VC). One single "US in Retreat" card when the US has Retreated. Cards are shuffled together face up (S8.1.2, S8.5.3).
- Scenario scope: Extended scenarios use the FitL bot (flowcharts or Trưng) up to the Talks and Tây Sơn after. Black April is Tây Sơn from the start. Black April NP may be ARVN or NVA (S8.2.1, S8.4.2).
- Card draw: move the top card to the bottom until the top card matches the active Faction. Read blue boxes: green arrow for true, red for false. Arrows may say draw a new card or flip the card (S8.5.3).
- Activation Number: a die roll that caps spaces per Operation. After each space roll 1d6. A roll at or below the number stops the Operation. Exceptions: NP US Patrol Sweep Assault, ARVN Patrol, NVA March onto LoCs, Armor placement, VC LoC Terror (S8.5.4).
- Priorities: four tables. Space Selection (one per Faction), Move, Pieces, Spearhead Armor Group (S8.1.2). Ties break at random (S8.2.1). Selection is by repeated filtering down the column (S8.5.6).
- Event rules: US and ARVN use unshaded text. NVA and VC use shaded text. Events are marked Critical, Performed or not executed. 1st Eligible plays only Critical. 2nd Eligible plays Critical or Performed. Effective Events table filters (S8.5.5).
- Pivotal triggers (S8.5.5): NP US at War plays on 2d6 at or below NVA Control + NVA Bases - War Weary. NP ARVN plays on 1d6 above ARVN Troops in Casualties. NP NVA plays when the Trail is 4 and Saigon is not NVA-controlled. NP VC plays on 2d6 at or below Available VC Guerrillas.
- Paris choices (S8.4.2): NP NVA always picks War. NP US picks War only if NVA Control + Bases is over 15 and 2d6 is at or below US Troops plus Bases in Casualties. Otherwise NP US Retreats.
- Resources: NP NVA and VC do not track them. NP US and ARVN track ARVN Resources only if the other COIN Faction is a player (S8.5.4).
- Solitaire: the player can win only at the final Coup in 1975. NP Factions can win at any Victory Phase (S8.3.1).
- Armor logic: NP ARVN and NVA build Armor Groups (Armor plus up to 2 Troops not needed for Control). At most 2 groups per Spearhead (S8.7.5, S8.7.6).
- Saigon focus: space priorities weigh Saigon heavily (Non-player Design Notes). NP NVA pushes Armor into the Delta and Saigon (Kevin Crooks note).
- Randomness: Activation rolls, 1d3 Armor placement, 2d6 and 3d6 gates, random tie-breaks (S8.2.1, S8.6.4, S8.7.6).

## Unique mechanics

- Armor as a new force type, with its own hit values, Capture and Destroy draw pool (S1.4, S1.4.5).
- Spearhead: a Special Activity that moves units and then fights in one action (S4.3.4, S4.4.4).
- Paris Peace Talks: a mid-game, score-driven War or Peace decision that can remove the US (S6.8).
- US Posture track: a level that sets what the US and ARVN may do after Retreat (S6.9).
- Anti-War and War Weary as subtracted scores (S7.2).
- Saigon as a condition of victory (S7.2).
- ARVN Casualties box with a Resource cost to recover pieces (S3.2.1, S6.8.2).
- A conventional-war 2-player scenario inside a COIN game (Black April). Herman calls it an experiment in going from guerrilla war to a "mobile" phase (Playbook p.32).
- Unilateral US Retreat choice (S6.1).

## Relevance

- Inferred: Armor, Spearhead and Captured Armor show how to add a conventional phase to an insurgency game. A PKK game could use a similar piece for a later, larger-unit stage. The 1984 to late 1980s span may not need it.
- Inferred: a score-driven Talks event with a forced-or-choice table (S6.8) is a reusable pattern for a negotiation or ceasefire step.
- Inferred: a subtracted score such as War Weary or Anti-War is a simple way to model domestic cost. Falling Sky could reuse it for a faction under strain.
- Inferred: the card-based bot rules (Activation Number, Armor Group lists, Saigon-weighted tables) are a template for a digital bot. The rule that bots skip illegal instructions and randomize at the end is easy to code.
