# Andean Abyss (COIN Volume I)

## Identity

- Title: Andean Abyss, COIN Series Volume I, Second Edition (1.0, rules p.1).
- Designer: Volko Ruhnke (rules cover). Developer: Joel Toppen (Playbook credits). Rules and charts: Mark Simonitch and Charles Kibler. Map and counters: Chechu Nieto and Xavier Carrascosa (Playbook credits).
- Rules file: Second Edition rulebook, (c) 2018 GMT Games. Playbook file: (c) 2012 GMT Games. Map art printed on the board: Chechu Nieto.
- Setting: Colombia, 1990s and early 2000s. Insurgent and counterinsurgent conflict (1.0).
- Scale: Departments, Cities and Lines of Communication. One Population point is about 1 million people (1.3.3, Key Terms: Population).
- Players: 1 to 4 (1.0, 1.5). Leftover Factions run as Non-players (1.5, 8.0).
- Components: 76 cards, 22 by 34 inch board, 153 wooden pieces, 8 cylinders, 3 dice (1.2).
- Scenario list: none in the rulebook. Setup is on the Sequence of Play aid sheet (2.1), which is not in the md file. Two options exist: Standard Deployment and Open Deployment (Key Terms). Scenario names: not verified.
- Length options: 4 Propaganda cards. Short Game Option ends at the 3rd Propaganda round (2.4.1).

## Factions

| Faction | Type | Pieces (total) |
|---|---|---|
| Government (Govt) | Government | 30 Troops, 30 Police, 3 Bases |
| FARC | Insurgent (Marxist) | 30 Guerrillas, 9 Bases |
| AUC | Insurgent (right-wing paramilitary) | 18 Guerrillas, 6 Bases |
| Cartels | Insurgent (narco-trafficking) | 12 Guerrillas, 15 Bases |

Source for counts: Available Forces chart (rules p.20, 1.4.1). Each Faction is enemy to all others (1.5).

**Victory conditions** (7.2). Checked at the start of each Propaganda Round (6.1).

| Faction | Win threshold | Victory margin (7.3) |
|---|---|---|
| Government | Total Support exceeds 60 | Total Support - 60 |
| FARC | Total Opposition + FARC Bases exceeds 25 | Total Opposition + FARC Bases - 25 |
| AUC | AUC Bases exceed FARC Bases | AUC Bases - FARC Bases |
| Cartels | More than 10 Bases and Resources above 40 | Lower of (Cartels Bases - 10) and (Cartels Resources - 40) |

- Total Support = 2 x Pop in Active Support + 1 x Pop in Passive Support. Total Opposition is the same for Opposition (1.6.3).
- Resources: each Faction holds 0 to 99 (1.7). Aid (0 to 29) adds to Government Resources only in the Resources Phase or by Event (1.7, 6.3.1).
- Ranking: if a Non-player passes a victory check, all players lose equally. Otherwise the highest margin ranks 1st. Ties go to Non-players, then Cartels, then AUC, then FARC (7.1).
- If one player runs two Factions, that player uses the lower margin and ends the game only if both Factions meet their conditions (1.5.1).
- Control: Government or FARC Controls a City or Department if its pieces alone exceed all others combined (1.8). Only these two Factions can Control.
- Special notes: Government cannot enter FARC Zones (6.4.4). Cartels need Resources, not only Bases. AUC wins by out-building FARC, not by map position.

## Sequence of play and eligibility

- No hands. Cards play one at a time. One card ahead is revealed (1.1, 2.2).
- Each Event card shows Faction symbols in order. The leftmost Eligible Faction is 1st Eligible (2.3.2).
- Eligible Factions may Pass, execute an Operation, or execute the Event. Ineligible Factions do nothing (2.3).
- Pass: gain +1 Resource (+3 for Government). The Faction stays Eligible. The next Faction in order becomes 1st or 2nd Eligible (2.3.3).
- 1st Eligible options: Operation with or without a Special Activity, or the Event (2.3.4).
- 2nd Eligible options depend on the 1st (2.3.4):

| 1st Eligible did | 2nd Eligible may do |
|---|---|
| Operation only | Limited Operation |
| Operation + Special Activity | Limited Operation or Event |
| Event | Operation, with Special Activity if desired |

- Limited Operation: one space, no Special Activity. Patrol, Sweep and March may draw pieces from many spaces but need one destination (2.3.5).
- Adjust eligibility: a Faction that executed an Operation or Event becomes Ineligible next card. Others become Eligible (2.3.7).
- Final Event card before the last Propaganda card: only Limited Operations, no Sweep, no March (2.3.9).
- Ship (2.3.6): a Faction that pays for an Operation but uses no Special Activity may remove one Shipment it owns. It then gets a free Limited Operation. Another Faction may remove its own Shipment to give this to the 1st or 2nd Eligible. Limit: once per card.
- Free Operations cost nothing and do not change eligibility for the Faction that did not play the Event (3.1.2, 5.5).
- Series change: none stated. This is the core COIN system. The Playbook notes that later volumes reuse it (Playbook, design notes).

## Operations and Special Activities

**Government Operations** (3.2)

| Operation | Cost | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Train | 3 per space | Place up to 6 cubes in each selected City or Dept with a Govt Base. In 1 space, replace 3 cubes with a Base or do Civic Action (3.2.1). |
| Patrol | 3 total | Move cubes onto LoCs and Cities. Activate 1 Guerrilla per cube on each LoC. Free Assault in 1 LoC (3.2.2). |
| Sweep | 3 per space | Move Troops in. Activate 1 Guerrilla per cube (1 per 2 cubes in Forest) (3.2.3). |
| Assault | 3 per space | Remove 1 Active Guerrilla per Troop. In City or LoC, Police also remove 1 piece each. In Mountain, 1 per 2 Troops. Bases last (3.2.4). |

- Drug Bust: each Shipment removed by Assault gives +6 Aid, to a maximum of 29 (3.2.5).

**Government Special Activities** (4.2)

- Air Lift: move up to 3 Troops from 1 space to another. Cost: none (4.2.1).
- Air Strike: with Patrol, Sweep or Assault. Remove 1 Active Guerrilla, or a Base only if the targeted Faction has no Guerrillas in the space. One Department or LoC, not a City (4.2.2).
- Eradicate: in 1 Dept with Cartels pieces. +4 Aid. Remove all Cartels Bases there. Shift that Dept or a neighbor 1 level toward Active Opposition if possible. Only if none can shift (all at Active Opposition or Pop 0), place 1 FARC Guerrilla in that Dept instead (4.2.3).

**Insurgent Operations** (3.3). All three Insurgents share the list.

| Operation | Cost | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Rally | 1 per space | Place a Guerrilla or replace 2 with a Base. With a Base: place Guerrillas up to Bases + Pop, or gather Guerrillas and flip them Underground. FARC avoids Support spaces. AUC avoids Opposition spaces (3.3.1). |
| March | 1 per City or Dept entered, 0 for LoCs | Move Guerrillas to adjacent spaces. They flip Active if the destination is a LoC or a City or Department with Support (AUC: Support or Opposition) and group plus cubes there exceed 3 (AUC counts FARC Guerrillas as cubes) (3.3.2). |
| Attack | 1 per space | Activate all Guerrillas. Roll a die. On a roll at or below Guerrilla count, remove up to 2 enemy pieces. On a 1, also add an Available Guerrilla. Separately, any Shipment removed goes to a space with a Guerrilla of the attacker (3.3.3). |
| Terror | 1 per City or Dept, 0 for LoCs | Activate 1 Underground Guerrilla. Place Terror and shift Support or Opposition 1 step toward Neutral (FARC: toward Active Opposition). Sabotage a LoC. AUC Terror cuts Aid by 3 (1 space) or 5 (2 or more) (3.3.4). |

**FARC Special Activities** (4.3)

- Extort: in spaces with an Underground FARC Guerrilla and FARC Control. Activate 1 Guerrilla, +1 Resource per space (4.3.1).
- Ambush: with Attack. Auto-success, no roll. Activates 1 Guerrilla only. Place a free Underground Guerrilla (4.3.2).
- Kidnap: up to 3 Terror spaces (City, LoC or Cartels Base space) with more FARC Guerrillas than Police. Roll a die and take that many Resources from Government or Cartels. A roll of 6 lets AUC place a piece. Drug Ransom takes a Cartels Shipment instead (4.3.3).

**AUC Special Activities** (4.4)

- Extort and Ambush: same as FARC (4.4.1).
- Assassinate: up to 3 AUC Terror spaces where AUC Guerrillas outnumber Police. Remove any 1 enemy piece in each, even a Base. Commandeer takes a removed Shipment (4.4.2).

**Cartels Special Activities** (4.5)

- Cultivate: with Rally or March. Relocate 1 Cartels Base into a Pop 1+ space where Cartels Guerrillas outnumber Police. Placing a new Base instead is allowed only in a Department selected for the Rally (4.5.1).
- Process: with Rally or March. Remove Bases for +3 Resources each, or place 1 or 2 Shipments under Guerrillas on Base spaces (4.5.2).
- Bribe: up to 3 spaces, costs 3 Resources per space. Remove up to 2 cubes, remove or flip up to 2 Guerrillas, or remove a Base. This is the only Special Activity with a cost (4.5.4).

## Event cards and deck

- Deck: 76 cards. Four are Propaganda cards. The rest are Event cards (1.2, Playbook tutorial).
- Event card parts: number, Faction order, title, flavor text, event text, lasting-effect indicator, shaded text (5.0).
- Flavor text is historical and has no effect (5.0).
- Dual Use (5.2): many Events have unshaded and shaded text. The executor picks one, whatever its Faction. Unshaded often favors Government. Shaded is often anti-Government. Dual Use reflects opposed effects, alternative history or alternative interpretations (5.2 design note).
- Govt Capabilities (5.3): marked Dual Use Events. A marker goes on the chosen side of the Govt Capabilities box. The effect lasts to game end.
- Insurgent Momentum (5.4): shaded text marked Momentum. The card sits in a holding box. It lasts until the next Reset Phase (6.6).
- Event rules: Event text beats rules, but never breaks availability, stacking, the Aid cap of 29 or the Resource cap of 99. Events cannot grant an Operation that is not available to the executor (5.1.1). The current Event beats an older one (5.1.2). Do what you can (5.1.3).
- Free Operations by Event do not cost Resources or change eligibility (5.5).
- Deck build: the exact build is on the setup sheet (not in the md file; not verified). The Playbook tutorial builds a stacked deck from 3 Propaganda cards with 16 cards per stack (Playbook, Tutorial). Another Playbook example deals the rest into 3 piles with Propaganda cards in 2 piles (Playbook, Example of Play). The rulebook says a Round never repeats back to back. Extra Propaganda cards play without a Round (6.0).
- Pivotal and Coup cards: none. Propaganda cards fill the Coup role.
- Historical content: titles include Plan Colombia, 1st Division, Hugo Chávez, Kill Zone, Sucumbíos, Narco-Subs and Darién (Playbook, Tutorial; 1.3.5). The Playbook has an Event text and background chapter and a source list (Playbook contents).

## Map, spaces and tracks

- Space types: rural Departments, Cities and LoCs. All can hold forces (1.3.1). Towns only separate spaces and are not spaces (1.3.6).
- Departments: Pop 0, 1 or 2. Terrain: Forest, Mountain or Grassland. Mountain hinders Assault. Forest hinders Sweep (1.3.2, 3.2.3, 3.2.4).
- Cities: Pop 1 to 8 (1.3.3). LoCs: Econ 1 to 3, Road or Pipeline (1.3.4). Total Pop 25 in Cities and 15 in Departments. Total Econ 30 (Playbook, Spaces List).
- Foreign countries are not spaces unless an Event says so, for example Darién and Sucumbíos (1.3.5).
- Adjacency: spaces that touch, Departments across a LoC, and LoCs or Departments across a Town (1.3.6).
- Coasts: spaces next to blue areas. They matter for "Narco-Subs" (1.3.7).
- Stacking: at most 2 Bases per Dept or City, none on LoCs. Govt forces cannot enter FARC Zones. Other pieces are unlimited (1.4.2).
- Support and Opposition: 5 levels from Active Support to Active Opposition. Only Pop 1+ Depts and Cities hold them. LoCs and Pop 0 Depts stay Neutral (1.6).
- Tracks (edge track and boxes): Resources and Aid, 0 to 99 and 0 to 29 (1.7). Total Support and Opposition + Bases on the same edge track (1.6.3). El Presidente track (6.4.3). Propaganda Card marker. Base tracks per Faction (1.4.1). Overflow boxes (1.4 play note).

## Propaganda / Coup / Winter round

Order of phases (6.0 to 6.6). Never run two Rounds in a row (6.0).

1. Victory Phase: check 7.2. If a Faction has won, the game ends. After the 4th card, use margins (6.1, 7.3).
2. Sabotage Phase: Sabotage each unSabotaged LoC where Guerrillas exceed cubes or an adjacent City is FARC Controlled (6.2).
3. Resources Phase (6.3):
   - Government: Econ of unSabotaged LoCs plus Aid. No Aid under Samper (6.3.1).
   - FARC and AUC: 1 per Base. Cartels: 3 per Base (6.3.2).
   - Drug Profits: owners remove Shipments for a Base or +6 Resources (6.3.3).
4. Support Phase (6.4): Civic Action (3 Resources per shift or Terror removal, Troops, Police and Control needed). Agitation (1 Resource each, FARC Control needed). Election: if President is Samper or Pastrana and Total Support is 60 or less, advance the track. Pastrana places a FARC Zone. Uribe removes all Zones. Elite Backing: AUC free Rally in 1 space with no Opposition, Govt Control or FARC Control (6.4.5).
5. Redeploy Phase: Government must move Troops off LoCs and Depts without Bases. It may move other Troops to Cities or Bases and Police anywhere Govt Controlled or to LoCs. Control updates after (6.5).
6. Reset Phase: all Factions Eligible. Remove Terror and Sabotage. Discard Momentum cards. Flip Guerrillas Underground. Play the next card (6.6).

- Final Round: do as much Civic Action and Agitation as possible. Skip Redeploy and Reset (6.6 play note).

## Non-player (bot) system

- Section 8.0 covers all four Factions. Flowcharts are on separate foldouts, not in the md file. The rulebook text summarizes them (8.1.3).
- Event or Operation: leftmost Insurgents play the Event. 2nd Eligible Insurgents play the Event unless the card shows a gray symbol (8.1). Non-players skip Events that have no effect (8.1). Insurgents under a Non-player Government use Event filters a to d (8.1, Insurgent Discord).
- Dual Use: Insurgents use shaded text, Government uses unshaded (8.4.1).
- Resources: Insurgents at 0 Resources Pass. Non-players never use Limited Operations or Shipping (8.1).
- "If none" arrows: if an action is not possible, follow the arrow to the next action (8.1.1).
- Priorities: numbered priorities are sequential, lettered ones are tie-breakers (8.1.3). Bases are placed before Guerrillas. Enemy removal order: Bases, Police, Troops, Underground, then Active (8.1.2).
- Randomness: ties between Cities or Departments use a 3-dice table (red column, yellow row, green space) (8.2). LoCs use highest Econ, then adjacency to a random City, then a die (8.3).
- Cartels: Rally, else March, else Terror. Then Cultivate, Process or Bribe (8.5).
- AUC: Rally, else March, else Attack, else Terror. Then Extort, Ambush or Assassinate (8.6).
- FARC: Rally, else March, else Attack (if FARC Resources exceed Government) or Terror. Then Extort, Ambush or Kidnap. Agitate in the Support Phase (8.7).
- Government: Pass if the next card is a Capability. Else Event, Assault, Train, Patrol or Sweep in that order of test. Add a Special Activity in a set order. Spending caps: Assault 9, Sweep 12, Civic Action floor 15 (8.8).
- 1-player: the player never wins in a Propaganda Round. The player must avoid any Non-player win and beat the best Non-player margin. Ranks: Failure, Stalemate, Progress, Triumph (8.9).
- Spare Faction options without bots: AUC runs Cartels with 3 players. Government runs AUC and FARC runs Cartels with 2 players (1.5.1).

## Unique mechanics

- Four Factions with one Government. Three Insurgents compete with each other as well as with Government (1.5).
- Shipments: drug markers that can be stolen, traded and used for a free Operation (2.3.6, 4.5.3).
- Underground and Active Guerrillas. Terror, Ambush and Extort need Underground Guerrillas (1.4.3).
- Bases last: Bases cannot be removed while friendly mobile pieces remain (3.2.4 note).
- El Presidente track: three Presidents change Aid and FARC Zones (6.4.3).
- FARC Zones: Departments closed to Government forces (6.4.4).
- Aid and Eradication: Government earns Aid by damaging Cartels, which helps FARC (4.2.3).
- Cartels victory needs both Bases and Resources (7.2).
- Transfers of Resources and Shipments between players are allowed (1.5.2).
- Non-player Insurgent Discord rule for 2-player games (8.1).

## Relevance

- Inferred: the Underground and Active Guerrilla flip, with Terror and Ambush tied to Underground status, fits a PKK game. It models hidden cadres against exposed ones.
- Inferred: one Government plus several Insurgents that also fight each other could model a Turkey game with PKK, rival Kurdish or leftist groups and the state. The tie-break order and "all players lose if a bot wins" rule could be reused.
- Inferred: the El Presidente track and FARC Zone idea could become a Turkish political-cycle track (election, martial law or emergency zones). Closed zones for state forces suit liberated mountain areas.
- Inferred: for a digital Falling Sky, reuse the section 8 style of ordered priorities, "if none" fallbacks, nested tie-breakers and a table-based random space pick. The Resources-per-space payment order (3.1 note) also suits a digital action planner.
