# The British Way: Palestine and Malaya (2nd Edition, 2026)

This file covers two of the four games in the multipack and the Campaign booklet. Kenya and Cyprus are covered by another file. Section numbers in parentheses point to the rulebook of the game in the section, unless the text names another source.

# Part A: Palestine

## Identity

- Title: The British Way: Palestine, one game in The British Way COIN multipack (1.0).
- COIN volume number: not verified. The rulebook does not print one. The Campaign booklet calls the multipack the first "COIN multipack" (Campaign booklet, Designer Notes).
- Credits: "Designed by Stephen Rangazas" (rulebook p.1). Game development: Joe Dewhurst. COIN Series design: Volko Ruhnke. Strategy Guide author: Joe Dewhurst (Campaign booklet p.26; Strategy Guide p.20).
- Edition: "2nd Edition", copyright 2026 GMT Games (p.1).
- Setting: British Mandatory Palestine, August 1945 to September 1947 (1.0). The game covers the fight against Irgun and Lehi. It does not model the wider Jewish Agency and Arab politics or the civil war that began in November 1947 (1.0).
- Scale: six Districts, three Cities and four Railways (1.3). One deck of 21 cards in play (2.1 setup).
- Players: two. British versus Irgun (1.5). Irgun stands for Irgun and Lehi together (1.5 Design Note).
- Scenario list: one, "Palestine Emergency: 1945-1947" (Setup Instructions). The Campaign booklet adds an optional Pre-War Options Table (Campaign 5.1).

## Factions

| Faction | Type | Pieces | Special resource |
|---|---|---|---|
| British | Government | 12 Troops (tan), 6 Police (blue) (1.4) | Intel Chits (9 total) (1.6.1) |
| Irgun | Insurgent | 15 Cells, 5 Arms Caches (1.2, 1.4) | Arms Caches (1.6.2) |

- No Resources track for either side (1.6).
- Victory is one shared track, British Political Will (PW), range 0 to 20 (1.8, 7.0). It starts at 18 (Setup).
- Irgun wins if PW is 0 at the victory check of any Propaganda Round (6.1.3, 7.0.1).
- British win if Irgun has not won at the end of the Political Will phase of the final Propaganda Round (6.1.4, 7.0.2).
- Intel Chits: values 0, 1 and 2, three of each, drawn at random from a bag. Chit values are open information. British spend a chit's full value in one space to remove Underground Cells with Patrol or Assault. They discard a chit of any value for Search or an Intelligence Lead (1.6.1).
- Cells are Underground or Active. Troops, Police and Arms Caches are always Active (1.4.3). Arms Caches: max two per space, never on Railways (1.4.2).
- Prison box: removed Cells wait there. Half of them return at Reset, rounded down (1.3.6, 6.4).
- Haganah is not a playable Faction. It is a track from 0 to 4 that gives or removes Irgun pieces and options (1.9, 6.5).
- Pieces are never taken from the map to fill an empty Available box (1.4.1).

## Sequence of play and eligibility

- One Event card is revealed at a time (2.2).
- Two-player Initiative Track with three boxes: Limited Operation, Event, Operation plus Special Activity (2.3.2).
- First Eligible chooses a box and acts. Second Eligible picks one of the two remaining boxes (2.3.2).
- Adjust Eligibility: the cylinder in the furthest left box becomes First Eligible (2.3.5).
  - Limited Op box guarantees First Eligible next card.
  - Op plus Special Activity box guarantees Second Eligible next card.
  - Event box depends on the other player (2.3.5 Design Note).
- Pass is allowed in any box. British Pass: draw one Intel Chit. Irgun Pass: Rob in one space (2.3.3).
- Passing in the Event box is the only way to block an Event (2.3.5 Design Note).
- Limited Operation: one space, no Special Activity. Deploy, Patrol and Search may use pieces from many spaces but only one destination (2.3.4).
- Operations normally cover up to three spaces (3.1). Extra space options:
  - Intelligence Lead: British discard one Intel Chit (2.3.7).
  - Offensive: Irgun return one Arms Cache from a space with no British (2.3.7).
  - Irgun get one extra space when Haganah is at 4 (3.1, 6.5.3).
- A Special Activity may happen before, during or after its Operation (4.1).
- Irgun start as First Eligible. Reset sets Irgun First and British Second (2.3.1, 6.4).
- Change from the series core: this sequence is a simplified two-player version of Colonial Twilight's (2.3 Note).

## Operations and Special Activities

British Operations (3.2):

| Name | Effect, cost, limits |
|---|---|
| Deploy | Up to 3 District or City spaces with more Troops than Irgun pieces get 1 Police each. Then move any Troops from anywhere to one space (3.2.1). |
| Patrol | Move any Police to up to 3 destination spaces with Cells. In one destination, remove 1 Active Cell to Prison per Police. Underground Cells need Intel value spent (3.2.2). |
| Search | Move adjacent Troops in. District or Railway: Activate 1 Cell per 2 cubes. City: place Curfew and Activate 1 per 3 cubes, or discard a chit to Activate 1 per cube with no Curfew (3.2.3). |
| Assault | In up to 3 spaces with Troops and Irgun, remove 1 Active piece per 2 Troops. Cells first, Arms Caches only when no Cells remain. Each Arms Cache removed gives +1 PW and 1 Intel Chit. Cells go alternately to Available and Prison (3.2.4). |

British Special Activities (4.2):

- Restore: only with Deploy. In one space with Troops, Police and no Irgun, remove Curfew and Sabotage and flip Terror to Sabotage (4.2.1).
- Negotiate: with Deploy, Patrol or Search. Roll a die, add 1 per Terror marker. If above the Haganah value, shift Haganah left. At 0, draw an Intel Chit instead (4.2.2).
- Mass Detention: with Search or Assault. In one City with Curfew and 3+ Troops, remove a die roll of Cells from the City and its adjacent District to Prison. Any excess lowers PW (4.2.3).

Irgun Operations (3.3):

| Name | Effect, cost, limits |
|---|---|
| Recruit | Up to 3 spaces without Curfew. With or next to an Arms Cache: up to 2 Cells. Otherwise only Cities, 1 Cell (3.3.1). |
| Travel | Move Cells from up to 3 origin spaces to any spaces. Cells go Active if the destination is a Railway or Curfew space and moving Cells plus cubes exceed 3. Otherwise they flip Underground (3.3.2). |
| Sabotage | Up to 3 spaces with an Underground Cell. Activate 1 Cell. Optionally spend an adjacent or local Arms Cache for +2. Roll a die, minus 1 per Police. Above 2 places a Sabotage marker (3.3.3). |
| Rob | Up to 3 Districts or Cities with more Cells than Police. Roll above 2 to add an Arms Cache. If Police are there, Activate 1 Cell (3.3.4). |

Irgun Special Activities (4.3):

- Silence: with any Irgun Operation. In one space with Underground Cells at least equal to Police, remove 1 Police (4.3.1).
- Propagandize: with any Irgun Operation. Up to 2 Cities with a Cell and a Curfew. Activate a Cell if all are Underground. Lower PW by 1 per space (4.3.2).
- Terror: only with Sabotage, in one City. A success places a Terror marker. A modified roll of 6 or more places two and shifts Haganah left (4.3.3).

## Event cards and deck

- 35 cards: P1 to P32 are Events, P33 to P35 are Propaganda (1.2, 9.0).
- Deck build: remove the three Propaganda cards. Make three piles of 6 Events. Shuffle one Propaganda card with 2 Events of each pile and put those 3 under 4 more Events. Stack the piles for a 21 card deck. 14 Events are unused (Setup Instructions).
- Dual Use: the player picks the unshaded or shaded text, whichever Faction he plays (5.2).
- Capabilities: eight cards (P9, P13, P17, P18, P23, P25, P29, P32). Effects last for the rest of the game. The BRITISH or IRGUN label is flavor only (5.3, 9.0).
- Starred Events (P2, P4, P8, P20, P27): PW loss is lowered by 1 if Ernest Bevin unshaded (P9) is in play (5.4, 9.0 P9 tips).
- Rule precedence: Event text beats rules, but not stacking, Available limits or the PW range (5.1.1).
- Content notes: each card has flavor text, tips and a Background note. Notes cite chapters of Bruce Hoffman's Anonymous Soldiers (9.0). Examples: Operation Agatha, King David Hotel, Exodus Affair, Acre Prison Break, Sergeants Affair, Dov Gruner, Roy Farran.

## Map, spaces and tracks

- Spaces: 6 Districts (Sabotage value 1), 3 Cities (Haifa, Tel Aviv-Jaffa, Jerusalem; Sabotage value 2), 4 Railways (1.3.2 to 1.3.4).
- District names from setup: Galilee, Haifa, Samaria, Lydda, Jerusalem, Gaza (Setup Instructions).
- Railways: Egypt to Tel Aviv-Jaffa, Tel Aviv-Jaffa to Jerusalem, Tel Aviv-Jaffa to Haifa, Haifa to Syria. Railways are separate spaces next to all spaces they touch (1.3.4, 1.3.5).
- Egypt, Transjordan, Syria and Lebanon are not playable (1.3.1).
- No Control, no Support or Opposition, no Population (Campaign booklet Quickstart; Designer Notes). Map numbers are Sabotage values (Quickstart).
- Tracks: Political Will 0 to 20. Haganah Track 0 to 4 (6.5; values named in the example of play and setup: 0 gives Intel, 2 Neutral, 3 Support, 4 Coordinate).
  - Entering 4 or 3 from below gives Irgun the pieces printed on the track. Leaving them takes pieces back, first from Available, then the map, then Prison (6.5.2).
  - Haganah rises only through Events. It falls through Negotiate, Terror and Events (6.5.1).
- Markers: 3 Curfew (one per City, removed when the City has no British pieces, by Restore, or at Reset), Sabotage and Terror (unlimited per space) (1.4.2, 1.10).

## Propaganda / Coup / Winter round

1. Political Will phase (6.1). Compute the net change first, then move the marker.
   - Minus 1 per Sabotage in a District. Minus 2 per Sabotaged Railway (once each). Minus 2 per Sabotage in a City. Minus 3 per Terror in a City (6.1.1).
   - Plus 2 if no Railway is Sabotaged. Plus 1 per City with no Sabotage or Terror (6.1.2).
   - PW 0 gives immediate Irgun victory. On the final card with Irgun not winning, British win (6.1.3, 6.1.4).
2. Resource phase (6.2). Irgun Rob in one space. British with no chits draw one, otherwise discard half rounded down. Haganah 0: British draw a chit. Haganah 4: Irgun place an Arms Cache in a space with a Cell.
3. Redeploy (6.3). British move Police to Railways or spaces with British pieces, and Troops to Cities or Railways. Irgun move one Cell to each City without Curfew and one Arms Cache to a space with a Cell.
4. Reset (6.4). Remove Curfew, Sabotage and Terror. Return half of Prison. Flip all Cells Underground. Irgun First Eligible.
- Three Propaganda Rounds in the game. Each campaign averages six Event cards (Strategy Guide).

## Non-player (bot) system

None in this rulebook. Both Factions are human players (1.5). The Campaign booklet and Quickstart mention no bot.

## Unique mechanics

- Intel Chits: a hidden-value random resource that gates attacks on Underground pieces (1.6.1).
- Underground Cells need paid intel to remove. Arms Caches can be removed only after all Cells (3.2.4).
- Prison box with partial return (1.3.6). Inspired by A Gest of Robin Hood (1.3.6 Design Note).
- Haganah Track adds or removes Irgun pieces and an Operation space (6.5).
- Railways as targets with a 4 point PW swing: -2 plus lost +2 (6.1; Strategy Guide).
- Curfew both blocks Recruit and enables Propagandize (1.10, 4.3.2).
- Travel selects origin spaces and may go anywhere (3.3.2).
- Sabotage needs a die roll (3.3.3).
- Mass Detention trades Prison removals for PW loss (4.2.3).

## Relevance

- Inferred: the Underground/Active status plus an intel currency fits a PKK game. Revealing cells is the hard problem, as in Palestine.
- Inferred: a single government will-to-fight track with scored attack markers by space value avoids a Support and Control model. This is useful if the PKK game focuses on spectacular attacks against state prestige.
- Inferred: curfews and mass detention that raise insurgent options but cost government will map to emergency rule and village-guard style policies.
- Inferred: a "patron" track like Haganah could model Syrian or Iraqi Kurdish support for the PKK.

# Part B: Malaya

## Identity

- Title: The British Way: Malaya, one game in The British Way multipack (1.0).
- COIN volume number: not verified.
- Credits: "Designed by Stephen Rangazas" (p.1). Developer Joe Dewhurst (Campaign booklet p.26).
- Edition: "2nd Edition", copyright 2026 GMT Games (p.1).
- Setting: British counterinsurgency against the Malayan Communist Party (MCP), June 1948 to July 1960. The game stresses the earlier stages and the rural peninsula. It does not model union struggles in Kuala Lumpur and Singapore (1.0).
- Scale: eight Provinces, Thailand, Kuala Lumpur and two Economic Centers (1.3.1).
- Players: two. British versus MCP (1.5).
- Scenarios: Malayan Emergency 1948-1960 (21 card deck) and Extended Malayan Emergency (27 card deck, two more cards per Campaign, optional) (Setup Instructions). The Pre-War Options Table "Labor Unrest" is in the Campaign booklet (5.2).

## Factions

| Faction | Type | Pieces | Resources |
|---|---|---|---|
| British | Government | 15 Troops, 15 Police (1.2) | 0 to 20 (1.8); start 20 |
| MCP | Insurgent | 15 Guerrillas, 4 Bases (1.2, 1.4) | 0 to 20; start 10 |

- Victory uses British Political Will (PW), range 0 to 20, start 12 (1.10; Setup).
- Early victory at any Propaganda Round: MCP if PW is 0 to 2. British if PW is 18 to 20 (6.1.3, 7.0.1).
- Final victory: MCP if PW is below 10. British if PW is above 10. Exactly 10 is a tie (7.0.2).
- Control: a Faction controls a space if its wooden pieces outnumber the opponent's. Gaining MCP Control lowers PW by the space's Population. Removing MCP Control raises PW by the same amount (1.7).
- Bases: max 2 per space, none in Economic Centers (1.4.2).
- Only the MCP may enter Thailand (1.3.3).
- Unlike Palestine, a Faction may take its own pieces from the map when none are Available (1.4.1).
- British Police stand for urban police and rural militias (1.4 Design Note).

## Sequence of play and eligibility

- Same Initiative Track and Limited Op / Event / Op plus Special Activity boxes as Palestine (2.3).
- British start First Eligible. Order is not changed during a Propaganda Round (2.3.1 Note).
- Pass: British gain 2 Resources. MCP gain 1 Resource (2.3.3).
- Limited Garrison, Sweep and March may use many origins but one destination (2.3.4).
- Operations may cover any number of spaces if the Faction can pay (3.1). This differs from Palestine's cap of three.
- Free Operations from Events cost no Resources. Pacify and Agitate still cost (3.1.2, 5.4).

## Operations and Special Activities

British Operations (3.2):

| Name | Effect and cost |
|---|---|
| Train | Pay 2 per space where cubes are placed (Pacify is paid separately). Kuala Lumpur: up to 4 cubes. Non-MCP Provinces: up to 2 Police each. In one space with British Control, Troops and Police, Pacify: 2 per Terror removed or one shift to Support (3.2.1). |
| Garrison | Pay 2 total. Move any cubes to Economic Centers, Kuala Lumpur and one Province. Activate 1 Guerrilla per cube in each Economic Center. Assault free in one Economic Center (3.2.2). |
| Sweep | Pay 2 per destination. Move adjacent Troops in. Activate 1 Guerrilla per cube. Jungle: 1 per 2 cubes (3.2.3). |
| Assault | Pay 2 per space. Remove 1 Active piece per Troop. Mountain: 1 per 2 Troops. Kuala Lumpur and Economic Centers: 1 per cube. Guerrillas first, Bases only when no Guerrillas remain. Each Base gives +1 PW (3.2.4; wording scrambled in file, checked with Example of Play). |

British Special Activities (4.2):

- Resettle: only with Train. One British Controlled Province without a New Village gets one and shifts once toward Opposition (4.2.1).
- Reprisal: with Garrison, Sweep or Assault. One Province with Troops: set to Neutral, add a Terror marker, move one Guerrilla to an adjacent space (4.2.2).
- Air Strike: with any Operation. Remove 1 Active Guerrilla in up to two Provinces with British forces (4.2.3).

MCP Operations (3.3):

| Name | Effect and cost |
|---|---|
| Rally | Pay 1 per space. Spaces without Support or New Village. Place 1 Guerrilla or replace 2 with a Base. With a Base: place Guerrillas up to Population plus Bases, or flip all Underground. Agitate in one MCP Controlled space with a Base (3.3.1). |
| March | Pay 1 per non-EC destination. Move Guerrillas into adjacent destinations. They go Active if the destination is an EC or Support and moving Guerrillas plus cubes exceed 3 (3.3.2). |
| Attack | Pay 1 per space. Activate all Guerrillas. Roll at or below Guerrilla count: remove up to 2 British cubes, Police first. Troops removed give -1 PW (3.3.3). |
| Terror | Pay 1 per non-EC space. Activate an Underground Guerrilla. Place Terror (Sabotage in an EC) if none there. Shift toward Opposition (3.3.4). |

MCP Special Activities (4.3):

- Extort: with Rally, March or Terror. Up to 2 spaces without a New Village. Activate an Underground Guerrilla for 1 Resource each (4.3.1).
- Intimidate: with Rally or Terror. Up to 2 spaces, each with an Underground Guerrilla. Remove 1 Police, or if Guerrillas outnumber British forces, remove a New Village and Support (4.3.2).
- Ambush: with Attack, in one paid Attack space. Activate one Underground Guerrilla, remove one enemy piece (Police first, Troops only once no Police remain), no roll. If a Troop is removed, Political Will falls by 1 (4.3.3).

## Event cards and deck

- 35 cards: M1 to M32 Events, M33 to M35 Propaganda (9.0).
- Deck: 3 piles of 6 Events, each with a Propaganda card shuffled into 2 Events, placed under 4 Events. 21 cards. Extended: 3 piles of 8, Propaganda mixed with 4, under 4 Events. 27 cards (Setup).
- Dual Use, Capabilities, precedence rules match Palestine (5.1 to 5.3).
- Capabilities in the text: M1, M2, M8, M18, M27, M28 (9.0).
- Free Operations: some Events grant them (5.4).
- Content notes cite Karl Hack's The Malayan Emergency (9.0). Examples: Batang Kali, Gurney Killed, Briggs-era New Villages, Templer-era Unity of Command, Special Branch, SEPs, Orang Asli, Ex-Palestine Recruits (M31).

## Map, spaces and tracks

- Spaces: eight Provinces, Mountain (Perak, Selangor) or Jungle (others). Kuala Lumpur (Population 2). Thailand (Population 0, MCP only). Two Economic Centers with value 6 each (1.3).
- Province names from setup: Perak, Johore, Pahang, Kelantan, Trengganu, Kedah, Selangor, Negri Sembilan (Setup Instructions).
- Population values 1 to 3 (1.3.2).
- Support/Neutral/Opposition in Provinces and Kuala Lumpur. Shifts move one step. Thailand and ECs are always Neutral (1.6).
- Control markers for British and MCP in Provinces, Kuala Lumpur and Thailand (1.7). ECs cannot be Controlled (1.3.5).
- Resources 0 to 20 per Faction (1.8). PW 0 to 20 (1.10).
- Commander Track: Gurney, Briggs, Templer. It moves one step right at each Reset (6.5, 6.6.1).
  - Gurney: Reprisal in two spaces. Briggs: Resettle in two. Templer: Pacify in two with Train (6.6.2).
  - Gurney Killed (M10) shaded cancels the track effect until Reset (6.6.3).
- Four New Village markers. They block Rally and Extort and stop Opposition counting at Propaganda. Removal: British Controlled Support space with no MCP; MCP and no British; Intimidate; Events (1.12).

## Propaganda / Coup / Winter round

1. Political Will phase (6.1). Net change before moving:
   - Hearts and Minds: Support Population minus Opposition Population (Opposition with New Village not counted). Add the difference (6.1.1).
   - First place Sabotage in each EC without one where Guerrillas outnumber British cubes. Then minus 1 per Sabotaged EC and minus 1 for Terror in Kuala Lumpur (6.1.2).
   - Victory check at 0-2 or 18-20 (6.1.3). Final card: check score (6.1.4).
2. Resource phase (6.2). British earn controlled Population plus Economic value of unsabotaged ECs. MCP earn 2 per Base on the map. Cap 20.
3. Support phase (6.3). British Pacify in up to two spaces with Control, Police and Troops (2 Resources per Terror removed or shift, max one shift per space). MCP Agitate in up to two spaces with MCP pieces and no British Control (1 Resource each).
4. Redeploy (6.4). British Troops in ECs or MCP Controlled Provinces must move to Kuala Lumpur or British Controlled Provinces. Police may move to ECs, Kuala Lumpur or British Controlled Provinces. Then adjust Control.
5. Reset (6.5). Remove Terror and Sabotage. Flip Guerrillas Underground. Shift Commander Track right. Support, Opposition and Control markers stay.

## Non-player (bot) system

None in this rulebook.

## Unique mechanics

- Commander Track: a historical timeline that changes Special Activity reach (6.6).
- Thailand sanctuary, closed to British pieces (1.3.3).
- Control changes move PW immediately (1.7).
- New Villages as markers that block insurgent actions and discount Opposition (1.12).
- Economic Centers that pay British income and hurt PW when sabotaged (6.1.2, 6.2.1).
- Reprisal as a coercion action that sets Neutral, places Terror and pushes a Guerrilla out (4.2.2).
- Pieces may be taken from the map if none are Available (1.4.1).
- Ambush avoids revealing all Guerrillas (4.3.3).

## Relevance

- Inferred: a Commander-style track would let a PKK game show changing state doctrine by phase, for example emergency rule, village guard and cross-border operations.
- Inferred: New Villages are a model for forced village evacuation. A PKK game could use one marker type that blocks insurgent recruitment and also lowers Support.
- Inferred: a sanctuary space that only the insurgent may enter fits Iraqi Kurdistan bases.
- Inferred: Control swings that change a win track at once make the player feel the cost of lost territory in the 1984 to late 1980s setting.

# Part C: Campaign booklet

- Contents: End of Empire Campaign rules, Campaign Event cards (E1 to E18), Pre-War Options Tables, comparative articles, Designer and Developer notes, bibliography, Quickstart (Campaign booklet p.1).
- Linked campaign: play Palestine, Malaya, Kenya and Cyprus in order. One player is British every game. The other is the Insurgent (Campaign 2.0).
- Imperial Prestige track 0 to 15, start 13. Colonial Policy: Stay the Course, Stand Firm or Concessions. The British choose after the first Propaganda Round and cannot change it in that game (Campaign 2.2, 3.0, 3.1).
- Game outcome Influence, Exit, Scuttle or Surrender depends on final PW (Campaign 3.2).
  - Palestine and Cyprus: Influence at PW 6+, Exit at 1 to 5, Scuttle at PW 0 in the last round, Surrender at PW 0 in rounds 1 or 2.
  - Malaya and Kenya: Influence at PW 18+, Exit 10 to 17, Scuttle 3 to 9, Surrender 2 or lower.
  - Prestige change per outcome depends on policy. Example: Stand Firm gives +2, +1, -3, -5. Concessions gives 0, -1, -1, -3 (Campaign 3.2).
- Campaign end: Prestige 11 to 15 Colonial Retrenchment. 5 to 10 Wind of Change (Historical). 0 to 4 Colonial Flight (Campaign 3.3).
- Campaign Events: one at game start, one after each of the first two Resets. Early deck for Palestine and Malaya, Late deck for Kenya and Cyprus. Some events have game-specific text (Campaign 4.1 to 4.3).
- Pre-War Options Tables: d6 setup variants per game. Palestine: Jewish Agency (1 to 6). Malaya: Labor Unrest (1 to 6). Results 3-4 are historical (Campaign 5.0).
- Articles (pp.12 to 20): A British Way?, The Firm Smack of Government, Thugs, Bandits and Gangsters, Reaching the Wind of Change.
  - Claims: hearts and minds were rare. Coercion and population control mattered most. Mass relocation worked in Malaya and Kenya because the insurgents had no external support and narrow bases. Curfews were weak in Palestine and Cyprus.
- Designer Notes (p.21): the single PW victory track replaces static victory sums; cell-based insurgents have no Support or Control; the design centers coercion and civilian victimization.
- Quickstart: Palestine and Cyprus have no Resources, no Control and no Support. Malaya is closest to older COIN volumes (Quickstart).

## Shared rules across the four rulebooks

Compared by grep of headings and key phrases only (not read in full for Kenya and Cyprus).

- Identical section skeleton 1.0 to 9.0 in all four: Introduction, Sequence of Play, Operations, Special Activities, Events, Propaganda Round, Victory, Example of Play, Event Tips and Background. Each also has Strategy Guide, Key Terms Index and Setup Instructions.
- Verbatim text found in all four: 2.3 Initiative Track and Eligibility notes; 2.3.4 Limited Operation; 2.3.5 Adjust Eligibility and its Design Note; 2.4 Campaign definition; 3.1.1 Pawns; 4.1.1 Accompanying Operations; 5.1.1 Event precedence; 5.2 Dual Use; 5.3 Capabilities; 6.1 net PW calculation note; 7.0 Victory structure with 7.1 and 7.2 lists; deck build from three 6-Event piles (21 cards).
- Shared mechanics: British Political Will 0 to 20 as the one victory track; Underground/Active status; Propaganda Round with phases Political Will, Resource, (Support), Redeploy, Reset.
- Shared by pairs:
  - Palestine and Cyprus: Intel Chits, Arms Caches, Curfews, Prison, Cells, no Resources track, no 5.4 Free Operations heading.
  - Malaya and Kenya: Control (1.7), Resources (1.8), Support or Loyalty (1.6), Support phase (6.3), 5.4 Free Operations, 3.1.2 Free Operations, a Commander or Pipeline track (6.6).
- Differences: 1.x item lists, factions, tracks (Haganah, Commander, Pipeline, International Opinion) and the Pass reward. Pass: Palestine draws a chit or Robs. Malaya gains Resources. Kenya shifts Pipeline or gains Resources. Cyprus draws a chit or Ambushes (2.3.3 in each).
- Kenya heading 4.3 Mau Mau Special Activities is merged into the body text in the md file. Cyprus 1.2 Components and 4.3 are also run into the body. Not a rules difference.

## Design lessons for a Turkey/PKK game (inferred)

- Inferred: use one asymmetric win track, like Political Will, only if the state has a clear fatigue measure. For the PKK case a track of state resolve and international image fits. Static victory sums need more factions.
- Inferred: model an urban cell insurgent and a rural guerrilla insurgent with different piece sets, as Palestine and Malaya do. A 1984 to late 1980s PKK is mostly rural with sanctuary, so Malaya's Base, Thailand and Control model fits better than Palestine's.
- Inferred: make intelligence a scarce, random resource for the state (Intel Chits) to show why large forces fail.
- Inferred: give the state coercive options (Reprisal, Mass Detention, resettlement) that win ground but cost legitimacy, as Palestine and Malaya do.
- Inferred: a Commander track or similar doctrine timeline keeps scenarios historical without scripted rules.
- Inferred: the Campaign booklet's point on external support (MCP and Mau Mau had none) is a warning. A PKK game must model Syrian and Iraqi sanctuary and support, so population-control success is not assured.
- Inferred: the single-card reveal and three-box Initiative Track reduce rules load and suit a digital implementation.
