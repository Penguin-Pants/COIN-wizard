# People Power (COIN Vol. XI): rules summary

## Identity

- Title: People Power. Philippines, mid-1980s (1.0).
- Series: Volume XI of the GMT COIN Series (1.0).
- Credits (Playbook p.41): Game Design Ken Tee. COIN Series Designer Volko Ruhnke. Game Development Eric Harvey and Jason Carr. COIN Series Developer Jason Carr. "Bonifacio" NP design Joe Dewhurst. "Jacquard" solitaire system designer Bruce Mansfield. Map by Eric Harvey and Mark Simonitch.
- Rules file: copyright 2022 GMT Games (rulebook footer). Designer notes dated September 2022 (Playbook p.37).
- Setting: 1981 or 1983 to 1986. A violent insurgency and a nonviolent pro-democracy movement lead to the 1986 "People Power" Revolution (1.0).
- Scale: 10 playable map spaces named in setup (see Map). One card per turn. Two or three Campaigns (1.1).
- Players: 1 to 3. All three Factions are always in play. Non-player (NP) rules run any Faction not held by a player (1.4, 8.1.1).
- Scenarios (Setup):
  - Standard: 1983 to 1986. Two Campaigns.
  - Extended: 1981 to 1986. Three Campaigns.
- Optional modules: Personalities (5.3) and Acts of Desperation (5.4).
- Playbook design note: the designer wanted an easy entry game for the series. The design moved from a 4-player to a 3-player model, and from two insurgents (NPA and Moro) to one (Playbook p.37).

## Factions

Each Faction is an enemy to the other two (1.4). There are no alliances. Resources move between Factions only by open negotiation (1.4.1).

| Faction | Type | Pieces | Start resources (Std / Ext) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Government (blue) | Government | Troops, Police (12 each), 6 Bases (1.5, back cover) | 18 / 12 |
| NPA (red) | Insurgent, violent | 16 Guerrillas, 6 Bases | 4 / 6 |
| Reformers (yellow) | Insurgent, nonviolent | 16 Activists, 6 Bases | 4 / 6 |

Victory conditions (7.1, 7.2). A Faction wins when its total exceeds the threshold at a Victory phase:

| Faction | Formula | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Government | Pop of Government-Controlled spaces + Patronage | exceeds 18 |
| Reformers | Pop of Opposition spaces + Reformer Bases on map | exceeds 12 |
| NPA | Pop of Resistance spaces + NPA Bases on map | exceeds 13 |

- Victory margin = total minus threshold (7.2).
- If no one wins at the final Victory phase, the highest margin wins. Ties go to Reformers, then NPA, then Government (7.3).
- Standard start totals: Government 18, Reformers 1, NPA 3. Extended: 14, 0, 1 (Setup).
- Resources run 0 to 40. Aid (0 to 40) feeds Government income. Patronage (0 to 40) counts toward Government victory (1.9).
- Special notes:
  - Reformers never gain Control. Their Active Activists and all their Bases only block Government or NPA Control (1.7).
  - NPA and Reformers share one pool of four Protest/Strike markers (1.8.2).
  - Government gains Aid by removing NPA Bases and loses Aid by hitting Reformers or using Enrich (3.2.3, 3.2.4, 4.2.1).

## Sequence of play and eligibility

- One Event card is revealed each turn. Faction order is printed on top of the card (2.3.2).
- Eligible Factions act in that order. Ineligible Factions are skipped (2.3.2).
- Options for 1st Eligible (2.3.3):
  - Operation plus Special Activity.
  - Operation only.
  - Limited Operation (one space, no Special Activity).
  - Event (either shaded or unshaded text).
  - Pass.
- Options for 2nd Eligible (2.3.3):
  - After an Op plus SA by 1st: Operation without SA, or Event.
  - After an Op only or Event by 1st: any Operation (with or without SA).
  - Always: Limited Operation or Pass.
- 3rd Eligible: Limited Operation or Pass only (2.3.3).
- Series change: three Factions act, not two. All three can act on one card (Playbook p.44, 2.3.3).
- Ineligibility (2.3.1, 2.3.6):
  - Faction that did a non-Limited Op or an Event is Ineligible on the next card.
  - Faction that did a Limited Op or Pass stays Eligible.
  - Limited Operation does NOT cost Eligibility. This is a key series change (Playbook p.44, 2.3.5).
- Pass gives +1 Resource, or +3 for Government (2.3.4). A passing Faction is replaced by the next Eligible Faction as 1st or 2nd.
- Limited Sweep and Assemble may pull pieces from many origins into one destination. Limited March uses one origin and may use many destinations (2.3.5).
- A Limited Op may be declared "Operation Only" to deny the Event to later Factions (2.3.3 Play Note).
- Only the current card is visible. The next card is hidden (Playbook p.44, 2.2).
- Free Operations from Events cost nothing and do not change Eligibility of a Faction other than the one that played the Event (3.1.5, 5.1.5).
- Election cards interrupt play and start an Election Round (2.4).

## Operations and Special Activities

Each Faction has 4 Operations and 3 Special Activities. SA costs nothing (4.1). Each space may be chosen once per Op and once per SA (3.1.1, 4.1).

**Government (3.2, 4.2)**

| Name | Cost | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Train | 2 per space | Select Cities and Countryside with Control or a Base. Place up to 4 cubes only in Cities and Countryside with a Base. In one Controlled space: replace 3 cubes with a Base, or Civic Action (2 per Terror removed and per shift, up to 2 shifts to Support; needs Control, 1 Troop, 1 Police, no Protest or Strike) (3.2.1) |
| Sweep | 2 per destination | Move any cubes anywhere on the map (not out of Protest or Strike spaces). Then Activate 1 Guerrilla per 2 Troops (Cities: per 2 cubes) (3.2.2) |
| Roundup | 2 per space | Remove Active Reformer pieces up to the Police count. Activists before Bases. Aid -1 per 2 pieces removed (3.2.3) |
| Assault | 2 per space | Remove Active NPA pieces up to Troops (Cities: all cubes). Guerrillas before Bases. Aid +5 per Base removed (3.2.4) |
| Enrich (SA, any Op) | free | One non-Manila space with Control and Support: Patronage + 2x Pop, set to Neutral, Aid -1 (4.2.1) |
| Reprisal (SA, with Roundup or Assault) | free | 1 or 2 Controlled spaces: set Neutral, add Terror, remove Protest or Strike, move 1 Guerrilla or Activist to an adjacent space (4.2.2) |
| Charm (SA, with Train or Sweep) | free (1 Patronage) | One Controlled space: Patronage -1, shift once to Support, Aid +3 (4.2.3) |

**Reformers (3.3, 4.3)**

| Name | Cost | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Recruit | 1 per space | Spaces without Support: place 1 Activist, or replace 2 Activists with a Base. With a Reformer Base: place Activists up to Pop + Bases (3.3.1) |
| Assemble | 1 per destination | Move Activists from adjacent spaces. Remove Terror in destinations. Remove any number of Protest markers anywhere on the map (3.3.2) |
| Persuade | 1 per space | Spaces with Activists and Guerrillas: remove 1 Guerrilla per 3 Activists. Flip Strike to Protest (3.3.3) |
| Protest | 1 per space | Spaces with Activists, no Terror or Strike: place Protest (Activists turn Active) if none is there and one is Available. Shift once to Opposition only if a Protest marker is now in the space (3.3.4) |
| Appeal (SA, any Op) | free | Spaces with Activists and no Protest: return Activists to Available for +2 Resources each (4.3.1) |
| Convert (SA, with Recruit, Persuade or Protest) | free | 1 or 2 spaces with Active Activists and Government pieces: remove 2 cubes or replace 1 cube with an Activist, or swap a bare Government Base for a Reformer Base. Patronage -1 per space (-2 for a Base) (4.3.2) |
| Canvass (SA, with Assemble or Persuade) | free | One space with Activists and Terror: set to Opposition, remove Terror (4.3.3) |

**NPA (3.4, 4.4)**

| Name | Cost | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Rally | 1 per space | Spaces without Support: place 1 Guerrilla, or replace 2 with a Base. With a Base: place Guerrillas up to Pop + Bases, or flip all Underground (3.4.1) |
| March | 1 per origin | Move Guerrillas to adjacent spaces as a group. Max 1 Guerrilla per origin into Visayas or Cebu. Guerrillas turn Active only in a Support space where group plus cubes exceeds 3 (3.4.2) |
| Attack | 1 per space | Activate all Guerrillas. Roll a die: at or under the Guerrilla count removes 2 Government pieces. Patronage -2 per Base removed. A roll of 1 adds a Guerrilla (3.4.3) |
| Terror | 1 per space | Needs an Underground Guerrilla. Activate 1. Remove Strike or Protest, place Terror, shift once to Resistance (3.4.4) |
| Extort (SA, with Rally, March or Terror) | free | Spaces without Government Control: Activate 1 Guerrilla, +1 NPA Resource each (4.4.1) |
| Strike (SA, with March only) | free | 1 or 2 Cities with Underground Guerrilla, no Terror: Activate 1, remove up to 1 Activist, place or flip to Strike, shift once to Resistance only if a Strike marker is present after placing or flipping (4.4.2) |
| Ambush (SA, with March or Attack) | free | One space with an Underground Guerrilla that just Marched or is about to Attack (an Ambush with Attack modifies that Attack; with March it adds a free Attack there): Activate 1 Guerrilla, remove 2 Government pieces with no roll, place a Guerrilla (4.4.3) |

## Event cards and deck

- 36 Event cards (#1 to #36), 4 Election cards (#37 to #40), 12 Personality cards (#41 to #52) and 9 Act of Desperation cards (#53 to #61) (1.2, Playbook p.22 to 35).
- Event cards are Dual Use. Each has unshaded and shaded text. The executor picks either (5.1.4).
- Unshaded text often favors Government. Shaded text often favors Reformers and NPA (5.1.4).
- Event text beats the rules, but may not break the 0 to 40 limits or the stacking limit (5.1.1).
- Deck build (Setup):
  - Shuffle the 36 Events. Deal 10 into each pile. Standard uses 2 piles. Extended uses 3.
  - Shuffle one Election card into the bottom four cards of each pile.
  - Stack the piles. Unused Events are set aside unseen.
  - Standard: 1984 (#39) on top pile, 1986 (#40) on bottom pile. #38 starts in the Election box. #37 is unused.
  - Extended: #37 starts in the Election box. Piles top to bottom use #38, #39, #40.
- Election cards give a Momentum effect for the whole Campaign (2.4.1, 5.2):
  - #37 1981 Presidential: Enrich adds only Pop, not double (Playbook p.29).
  - #38 Ninoy Aquino Murdered: Protest sets a space directly to Opposition (Playbook p.30).
  - #39 1984 Parliamentary: Reformer Limited Ops cost 0. Reformer Pass gives +2 (Playbook p.30).
  - #40 1986 Snap Election: triggers the final Election Round (Playbook p.30).
- Personality cards (#41 to #52): 4 per Faction. Each gives a Momentum effect for one Campaign. Examples: Enrile (spend 4 Resources for 3 Patronage), Cory Aquino (extra Activist per City in Recruit), Sison (Rally may replace 1 Guerrilla with a Base in Countryside), Salas (Ambush in 2 spaces) (5.3, Playbook p.31 to 33).
- Acts of Desperation (#53 to #61): 3 per Faction. Each has a condition checked at the final Victory phase. If the condition fails, the card does nothing (5.4). Examples: People Power (3+ Protests: Patronage -2), National Strike (1+ Strike: Patronage -2), Enrile and Ramos Defect (swap a Government Base for a Reformer Base in an Opposition space) (Playbook p.34 to 35).
- Historical content: each card has flavor text and a Playbook background note (Playbook p.22 to 35). Pre-1983 and post-1983 cards can appear out of order. The Playbook gives rules for reading such cases (Playbook p.21).
- No Capabilities or Pivotal Event cards exist. Momentum comes from Election and Personality cards only (5.2).

## Map, spaces and tracks

- Spaces are Cities (round) and Countryside (irregular). South China Sea and Sulu Sea are not playable (1.3.1).
- Population is 1, 2 or 5 (1.3.1). Adjacency is a shared white border (1.3.2).
- Spaces named in setup: Manila (City), Cebu (City), Zamboanga (City), Davao (City), Northern Luzon, Southern Luzon, Visayas, Western Mindanao, Central Mindanao, Eastern Mindanao (Setup). The NP example speaks of "three out of four Cities" (8.11), so four Cities exist. Which spaces are Cities is inferred: Manila, Cebu, Zamboanga and Davao. Pop values per space are not verified (no map image).
- Adjacency examples given: Manila touches Northern and Southern Luzon. Southern Luzon touches Manila, Northern Luzon and Visayas. Northern Luzon does not touch Visayas (1.3.2).
- Alignment (1.6): each space is Neutral, Support (Government), Opposition (Reformers) or Resistance (NPA). Only one level each.
  - A shift from a held alignment first goes to Neutral. A second shift places a new alignment (1.6.2).
  - "Set" effects change alignment directly (1.6.3).
- Control (1.7): Government or NPA Controls with more pieces than the other two Factions combined. All Bases count. Activists count only in Protest spaces.
- Stacking (1.5.2): max 2 Bases per space. One marker only of Terror, Protest or Strike per space. Overflow boxes exist (1.3.3).
- Markers (1.8):
  - 10 Terror markers.
  - 4 Protest/Strike markers (double-sided).
  - Terror blocks Protest and Strike. Placing Terror removes them.
- Edge track (0 to 40): Resources, Aid, Patronage and victory totals (1.9, 1.10).
- Election Card box holds the current Momentum card (2.4).
- Bases Last rule: remove non-Base pieces before Bases (1.5.4).

## Propaganda / Coup / Winter round

People Power calls this the Election Round. It runs when an Election card appears (6.0). Phases in order:

1. Victory phase (6.1):
   1. Strike!: Patronage -1 per Strike marker on map (6.1.1).
   2. Acts of Desperation (final round only): resolve from lowest margin to highest (6.1.2).
   3. Victory check: if any Faction exceeds its threshold, game ends (6.1.3, 7.1).
   4. Game end?: in the final round, game ends and highest margin wins (6.1.4, 7.2).
2. Resources phase (6.2): Government gets Aid + Pop of Controlled spaces, then Aid resets to 5. Reformers get Pop of Opposition spaces. NPA gets 2 per NPA Base. Max 40.
3. Support phase (6.3):
   - Civic Action: in Controlled spaces with Troops and Police, 2 Resources per Terror, Protest or Strike removed, or per shift to Support. Protest or Strike must go first.
   - Agitation: in NPA-Controlled spaces, 1 Resource per Terror or Protest removed, or per shift to Resistance.
   - Foundation: Reformers place one Base in one space without Government Control.
4. Redeploy (6.4): Government only. Police move to any Controlled space. All Troops must end in Controlled Cities, spaces with a Government Base, or Manila.
5. Newsmakers (6.5): remove old Personalities. Each Faction picks a new one. On the 1984 round, each Faction picks an Act of Desperation.
6. Reset (6.6): remove all Terror, Strike and Protest markers. Flip all Guerrillas Underground and Activists Inactive. Place the Election card in its box. All Factions become Eligible. Reveal the next card.

- Standard has two Election Rounds. Extended has three (6.0).
- The victory check happens first in every Election Round, so a Faction can win early (6.1.3).

## Non-player (bot) system

"Bonifacio" runs any Faction (8.1.1). It follows the Jacquard family (Arjuna, Trung, Tay Son) with changes for three Factions (8.1.1).

- Components (8.1.2): 6 cards per Faction (18 total), three priority tables (Space Selection per Faction, Move, Pieces), Eligibility table, Effective Events table, Election Round instructions.
- Golden rules (8.2.1): NP follows rules, but never removes own pieces from map to Available, tracks no Resources, and uses an Activation Number. Skip illegal instructions. Do what the text says, no more. When in doubt, randomize.
- Action choice (8.5.2, 8.5.5): NP plays an Event only if it is Critical (shaded icon behind its Faction symbol) and Effective (matches the Effective Events table). Otherwise it does Op plus SA, or a Limited Op.
  - A 2nd Eligible NP often takes a Limited Operation to stay Eligible (8.12).
- Event text: NP Government uses unshaded. NP NPA and Reformers use shaded (8.5.1).
- Card flow (8.5.3): draw the next card for the active Faction. Cards go to the bottom of the deck, face up. Read from the top: green arrow if condition true, red if false. Flip to the double-letter side or redraw as arrows say. Operation and SA appear in a fixed order of priority. If no SA is possible, take a Limited Op or Op only.
- Activation Number (8.5.4): after each space, roll 1d6. A roll at or under the number on the card stops further spaces. Higher adds another space. NP Reformers use a number one lower in the final Campaign. Rally and Recruit Base placement and free Event Ops skip the roll.
- Resources (8.5.4): NP never gains or spends them. NP NPA and NP Reformers still use Extort and Appeal as "wasted" Special Activities to block others (8.5.4 design note).
- Space priorities (8.5.6): read a column of dotted rows. Each row narrows the candidate set. Repeat until one space is left. Ties are random.
- Moves (8.5.7): choose destination first, even for NPA March. Then pick an origin with most pieces. "Keep" and "get" rows set how many pieces stay and go. Government keeps Control of 2+ Pop spaces.
- Pieces (8.5.8): read the table top down to place, move or remove enemy. Read bottom up to remove own pieces.
- Personalities and AoD (8.6, 8.10.5): selection uses die-roll priority lists, for example NPA picks Sison if 1d6 is at or under Available NPA Bases. AoD choice depends on which other Factions are NP.
- Election Round (8.10): Civic Action and Agitation use up to 1d6 steps. Foundation uses Place Bases column. Redeploy follows a 5-step list. NP gets no Resources.
- Victory (8.3): in solitaire, the player may only win at the final round. NP can win at any Election Round.
- Targeting (8.12): NP Factions prioritize a human player. Two NP Factions will gang up on a human. Some priority rows run only against a player Faction.
- Example of play (8.11): ten cards plus an Election Round, with all three Factions as NP.

## Unique mechanics

**Three-way triangle (Playbook p.18)**
- Government beats NPA, NPA beats Reformers, Reformers beat Government.
- Government is stronger than either rival alone.
- Government gets +5 Aid per NPA Base removed, but loses Aid for Roundup and Enrich (3.2.3, 3.2.4, 4.2.1).

**Nonviolent insurgent (Reformers)**
- Activists are Inactive (hidden from Roundup, ignored for Control) until a Protest marker is placed (1.5.3, 1.7).
- A Protest does not automatically block Government Control. It makes Reformer Activists count in the Control comparison, so Government loses Control only if its pieces no longer exceed the other two Factions combined. It also blocks cubes leaving the space and activates Activists. This makes the pieces visible and weak at once (1.5.3, 1.8.2, 3.2.2).
- Reformers cannot Recruit where Support exists, but can Assemble in freely (3.3.1, 3.3.2).
- Convert turns Government pieces into Activists and drains Patronage (4.3.2).
- Reformers never Control a space (1.7).

**Patronage as a victory term and a cost**
- Government victory counts Patronage with Control (1.10).
- Government raises it by Enrich (removes Support, may cut Aid) and lowers it by Charm. Reformer Convert and NPA Attack on Bases also lower it (4.2.1, 4.2.3, 4.3.2, 3.4.3).
- Strikes cost Patronage at each Election Round (6.1.1).

**Aid from a foreign patron**
- Aid is a US-linked lever: +5 per NPA Base removed, -1 per two Reformers removed, -1 per Enrich, +3 per Charm. It feeds Resources then resets to 5 (3.2.3, 3.2.4, 4.2.1, 4.2.3, 6.2.1).

**Insurgent-side details**
- Guerrillas flip Active or Underground one at a time. Underground status gates Terror, Strike, Extort and protects Bases (1.5.3, 3.4.4, 4.4).
- NPA March pays per origin. Government Sweep and Reformer Assemble pay per destination (3.4.2, design note).
- Sweep is not limited by adjacency. Government can airlift cubes anywhere (3.2.2 design note).
- Attack is a die roll against Guerrilla count. Ambush skips the roll (3.4.3, 4.4.3).
- Visayas and Cebu allow only 1 Guerrilla per origin by March (3.4.2).
- Terror is shared. Government Reprisal and NPA Terror both place it. It blocks Protest and Strike, and raises cost of Civic Action and Agitation (1.8.1).
- Civic Action in the Election Round can clear Protest and Strike, at 2 Resources each (6.3.1).

**Insurgent economy and bases**
- NPA earns 2 per Base in the Election Round, plus Extort in play (6.2.3, 4.4.1).
- Base replaces 2 Guerrillas. NPA Base loss costs Resistance score and gives Government Aid (3.4.1, 3.2.4).
- Control is a three-way comparison, not a two-way one (1.7).

**Sequence of play**
- Three Factions on each card; Limited Op keeps Eligibility; all three can act on one card (2.3.3, 2.3.5).
- Election cards act as scoring, income and win checks at once. Victory is checked before income (6.1.3, 6.2).
- Limited Sweep then Assault is a main Government combo for an NPA Base (Playbook p.18).

**Short game and endgame**
- Only 2 or 3 Campaigns (1.1).
- Acts of Desperation: hidden-info option, one chosen per Faction, resolved by lowest margin first (5.4, 6.1.2, 6.5.2).
- Personalities rotate each Campaign. Each can be used once (5.3, 6.5.1).

**Negotiation**
- Open, binding only for the current action (1.4.1).

**Bot design**
- Activation Number roll caps spaces instead of resource budgets, since NP tracks no Resources (8.5.4).
- A 2nd Eligible NP takes Limited Ops in preference to weak Events (8.12).

## Relevance

- Inferred: the Reformer model (pieces that are safe while quiet and exposed when they act) could model a legal or cultural Kurdish party in a PKK game. The 1980s Turkish setting has a violent group and a political group under a state that treats both as threats.
- Inferred: the triangle (state beats armed group, armed group beats political group, political group beats state) is a template for a three-Faction PKK game with state, PKK and a legal movement.
- Inferred: the Aid and Patronage levers could stand for foreign support and state clientelism. Terror as one shared marker that hurts both civil movements and the state is reusable.
- Inferred: for a digital Falling Sky, the Bonifacio design is useful: a Critical-and-Effective Event test, an Activation Number roll to cap spaces, and "get/keep" move rows are easy to code. The note that NP assumes a human is stronger and gangs up on that player is a clean balance rule.
