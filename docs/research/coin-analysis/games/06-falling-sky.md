## Factions

| Faction | Type | Victory condition (exact) |
|---|---|---|
| Romans (red) | Government | Subdued + Dispersed + Roman Allied Tribes exceeds 15. Margin = that sum minus 15 (7.2, 7.3) |
| Arverni (green) | Insurgent | Both: off-map Legions exceed 6 AND Arverni Allies + Citadels exceed 8. Margin = lower of (off-map Legions - 6) and (Arverni Allies + Citadels - 8) (7.2, 7.3) |
| Aedui (blue) | Other (pro-Roman Gauls) | Aedui Allies + Citadels exceed those of each other Faction (Gallic, Germanic, Roman; Forts not counted). Margin = Aedui total minus that of the Faction with the most (7.2, 7.3) |
| Belgae (yellow) | Insurgent | Belgic Control Value + Belgic Allies + Citadels exceeds 15. Margin = sum minus 15 (7.2, 7.3) |
| Germans (black) | System-run, no victory | Not a player. Does not track Resources or victory (1.5, 1.8, 7.0) |

Victory details:
- Off-map Legions = Fallen box + Legions track + removed by Event (1.9).
- Belgic Control Value = Control Value of every Region under Belgic Control, +1 for a Colony Region, -1 for each non-Suebi Dispersed Tribe. Control Value is counted by Region, regardless of Allies (7.2 play note).
- Roman score = 30 minus non-Roman Allies and Citadels on map, or 31 with Colony (7.2 play note).
- Aedui Allies are shown on the Aedui Available display, compared with the "Other Most Allies" marker (1.9).

Resources: each Roman and Gallic Faction holds 0 to 45 on the edge track (1.8). Germans have none.

Pieces (Playbook p.48; Forces shown on 1.4):
- Romans: 1 Caesar, 12 Legions, 20 Auxilia, 6 Allies, 6 Forts.
- Arverni: 1 Vercingetorix, 35 Warbands, 10 Allies, 3 Citadels.
- Aedui: no Leader, 20 Warbands, 6 Allies, 2 Citadels.
- Belgae: 1 Ambiorix, 25 Warbands, 10 Allies, 1 Citadel.
- Germans: 15 Warbands, 6 Allies, no Leader.

Other notes:
- Leaders: Caesar, Vercingetorix, Ambiorix. A removed Leader returns as a Successor during Spring (1.4.3, 6.6).
- Auxilia and Warbands are Hidden or Revealed. Legions, Leaders, Allies, Forts and Citadels are always Revealed (1.4.3).
- Two-player and three-player play: one player may run two Factions. Use the lower victory margin of the two. Exception: a single player running both Arverni and Belgae in The Great Revolt uses the higher (1.5.1, 2.1).
- Transfers: up to 4 Resources per execution between Factions of one player. Free transfers to non-German Factions during Quarters and Harvest (1.5.2).

## Sequence of play and eligibility

- One Event card is played and the next card is shown (2.2, 2.3.7).
- Eligibility: a Faction that did not execute a Command or Event last card is Eligible. One that did is Ineligible. All start Eligible (2.3.1).
- Order: Factions follow the left-to-right Faction symbols on the card. The leftmost Eligible is 1st Eligible, the next is 2nd Eligible (2.3.2).
- 1st Eligible may Pass, execute a Command (with or without a Special Ability) or the Event (2.3.4).
- 2nd Eligible options depend on the 1st (2.3.4):

| 1st Eligible did | 2nd Eligible may |
|---|---|
| Command only | Limited Command |
| Command plus Special Ability | Limited Command or Event |
| Event | Command, with Special Ability if wanted |

- Limited Command: one Region, no Special Ability, still counts as a Command (2.3.5).
- Pass: stays Eligible. Gain +1 Resource if Gallic, +2 if Roman. The next leftmost Eligible becomes 1st or 2nd Eligible. If the rightmost Eligible Passes, adjust and play the next card (2.3.3).
- Adjust after the card: Factions that did nothing (or were not made Ineligible by an Event) go Eligible. Factions that executed a Command or Event go Ineligible. Exceptions: Events and free actions (2.3.6, 3.1.2, 5.4).
- Frost: on the last Event card before each Winter, Commands from the Sequence of Play cannot use March. This covers Germanic Commands via Enlist. Events may still March. It does not affect the Germans Phase (2.3.8).
- Sudden Frost option (by agreement at setup): on the last Event card before Winter, swap the two showing cards and run an immediate Winter Round (2.3.8).
- Differences from the series core: Faction order is left to right by symbol, Limited Command rules are the usual ones, Winter replaces Propaganda or Coup, Frost is new, and only 72 Event cards exist so the Germans act by Winter, Enlist and Events, not by card order (2.3.8, 6.2; Playbook p.28).
- Non-players get full Commands plus a Special Ability option when 2nd Eligible (8.1.2).

### Battle procedure (3.2.4, 3.3.4)

Steps in each selected Region, one at a time:
1. Target: Attacker picks exactly one enemy Faction as Defender.
2. Declare Retreat: Defender says whether it will Retreat. Not allowed if Germanic, if it has only Allies, Citadels (Gallic Battle also Forts), or if Ambushed. Retreat to a Region under another Faction's Control needs that Faction's agreement now. Germans never Retreat or agree.
3. Attack: Attacker causes Losses.
4. Counterattack: skipped if Defender declared Retreat. Otherwise surviving Defenders cause Losses on the Attacker.
5. Reveal: skipped on Retreat. Otherwise all surviving Hidden Warbands and Auxilia of both sides flip Revealed.
6. Retreat: Defender moves to one adjacent Region under its own Control or that of an agreeing Faction (not No Control). Roman Attack: Leader and Warbands move or are removed. The Defender may let the Leader and Hidden Warbands stay put (3.2.4). Gallic Attack: Leader, Warbands, Auxilia and Legions must move or be removed, none may stay. Allies, Citadels (and Forts) stay.

Losses:
- Count = 1/2 per enemy Warband or Auxilia, +1 per Leader, +1 per Legion.
- Caesar Attacking: Defender takes 2 per Legion. Ambiorix Attacking: Defender takes 1 per Belgic Warband.
- Halve the total (round down after) if Defender Retreats or has a Citadel (Roman Attack; Citadel counts even if removed by Besiege) or a Citadel or Fort (Gallic Attack).
- Defender chooses which pieces go. A Legion, Citadel, Fort or Leader may take a Loss by die roll: 1-3 removed, 4-6 absorbed. Repeat rolls allowed. Legions go to Fallen.
- Retreating Defender takes Allies first, then (by roll) Citadel or Fort. Otherwise Allies, Citadel and Fort go only after all other pieces.
- Provincia Fort never absorbs Losses (1.4.2).
- Germans lose Scouted, then Revealed, then Hidden Warbands, then Allies (Cities last, otherwise random) (3.4.5).

Germanic Battle (3.4.4): Germans Battle only if they can Ambush (more Hidden pieces than the enemy) and always Ambush. Skip Steps 2 and 6. Target is usually random. Defender removes pieces equal to 1/2 per German Warband (half that with a Citadel or Fort, round down), with no 1-3 roll. Romans with Caesar in the Region roll: on a 4-6 they may absorb by roll, and Caesar counterattacks (Germans lose 1/2 per Auxilia, +1 Caesar, +1 per Legion).

### Germans

Germanic Commands cost 0 (3.4). Rally places an Ally only where the Germans have Control (never at Bibracte or Gergovia), then Warbands up to Germanic Allies (3.4.1). March uses one group per origin (3.4.2). Raid takes Resources from the target but the Germans gain none (3.4.3).

## Event cards and deck

- 72 Event cards plus 5 Winter cards = 77 (1.2, Playbook p.46). Scenarios use a subset.
- Deck build: separate the 5 Winter cards. Shuffle Events and deal piles of 5. Shuffle one Winter card into the piles named by the scenario. Stack piles top to bottom. Remaining cards stay unseen (Setup p.28).
- Card shows Faction order symbols, title, flavor text, unshaded and shaded text (5.0, 5.1).
- Non-player cues on Faction symbols: laurels (special instruction), crossed swords (that Non-player declines the Event), carnyx at Arverni (Non-player Arverni execute without rolling) (8.2.1).
- Dual Use: most cards have unshaded and shaded text. Executor picks either, never both, regardless of Faction (5.2).
- Event versus rules: Event text wins, except it never places unavailable pieces (remove instead of replace), never breaks stacking (unless text says so: Colony, Gallia Togata, Gobannitio), never raises Resources above 45, Reveal and Hide affect only Warbands and Auxilia, and Senate shifts at Firm flip the marker (5.1.1).
- Dueling Events: the currently played Event wins (5.1.2). Partial execution: do all that can be done (5.1.3).
- Capabilities (5.3): lasting effects. Put the card near the most affected player, place a Capability marker on the shaded or unshaded side. Effect lasts for the rest of the game unless a later Event alters it. Capability mark on the boundary means both versions are Capabilities.
- Playbook marks these cards as Capabilities (side per card not verified): Baggage Trains, Ballistae, Titus Labienus, Balearic Slingers, Legio X, Aquitani, Massed Gallic Archers, Vercingetorix's Elite, Diviciacus, River Commerce, Convictolitavis, Commius, Germanic Horse, Winter Campaign (Playbook pp.32-45).
- Free actions (5.4): Event-granted Commands and Special Abilities cost nothing and leave Eligibility unchanged. A free "Special Ability" without a named one may be any Special Ability. Besiege and Ambush need an accompanying Battle (5.4). "Ambush" alone grants an inherent Battle.
- Winter-linked cards: Optimates (keep by Winter track; at the 2nd and each later Victory Phase, if Roman victory exceeds 12, remove all Legions to the Legions track, end the game and determine victory), Sacking (Razed marker on a City), Colony (adds a 31st Tribe), Winter Campaign (shaded Capability gives 2 Commands after each Harvest) (Playbook pp.31, 36, 45).
- Historical content: Each card has Caesar quotes and background notes, with references to the Commentaries in [book.chapter] form (Playbook p.32).
- Germans act from Events too, for example Segni and Condrusi or Germanic Allegiances (Playbook p.44).

## Map, spaces and tracks

- 16 Regions: Belgica (Morini, Nervii, Atrebates), Germania (Sugambri, Ubii), Celtica (Treveri, Veneti, Carnutes, Mandubii, Pictones, Bituriges, Aedui, Sequani, Arverni), plus Provincia and Britannia (1.3, setup lists). See `boards/descriptions/falling-sky.md`.
- 30 Tribe circles. Some Tribes hold a City that may take a Citadel (1.3.2, 1.3.3).
- Stacking (1.4.2): one Ally per Tribe circle, one Citadel per City (replaces Ally), one Fort per Region. Provincia always has a permanent Fort. No Allies on Dispersed Tribes. Aedui, Arverni and Suebi circles are Faction-locked.
- Control (1.6): the Faction with more pieces than all others combined Controls a Region. Else No Control. Germans can Control.
- Tribe status: Subdued (empty), Allied, Dispersed. Seize makes Dispersed markers (max 4 on map). Dispersed blocks Allies and removes the Tribe's Belgic Control Value (1.7, 3.2.3).
- Britannia: adjacent to three coastal Regions across the channel. No Special Ability with March in or out (1.3.4).
- Rhenus: river border of Germania. Stops Marching Romans and Vercingetorix's second-Region move (1.3.5, 3.2.2).
- Cisalpina and Aquitania: not playable (1.4.2).
- Overflow box for crowded Regions (1.3.6).
- Edge track 0-45: Resources and victory markers. Senate and Legions track: three Senate rows (Uproar, Intrigue, Adulation), Legions, Fallen Legions box, Firm flag (1.4.1, 6.5).
- Markers: Devastated, Dispersed and Dispersed-Gathering, Scouted, Frost, Control, Capability, Razed, Colony, Overflow (1.3.6, 3.2.3, 4.2.2, 4.3.2, 5.0).

## Propaganda / Coup / Winter round

Triggered by each Winter card. Mark phases with the Winter marker (2.4, 6.0).

1. Victory Phase (6.1). If any Faction meets its condition, the game ends (exceptions: spare Factions, 54 BC 1st Winter, 1-player rules). If this is the final Winter, the game ends and 7.3 decides.
2. Germans Phase (6.2):
   - Rally: Germanic Allies first (Suebi, then Germania, then elsewhere), then Warbands (including Germania with no Ally). Random among equals.
   - March: each Region with Germanic Warbands beyond Control needs sends one group of as many as possible without losing Control, largest first. Destinations: add German Control, then player-Controlled, then others. Random among equals. Then flip all Germanic Warbands Hidden.
   - Raid: with as many Warbands as able against Factions with more than 0 Resources and no Fort or Citadel, until 0. Players before Non-players.
   - Battle with Ambush: where Hidden Germans exceed enemy Hidden pieces and a Loss is possible. Players before Non-players. Random order.
3. Quarters Phase (6.3):
   - Germans: Warbands in Devastated Regions without German Allies, outside Germania, move to Germania. Roll: 1-3 Sugambri, 4-6 Ubii.
   - Gauls (Belgae, Aedui, Arverni in that order): may relocate Warbands and Leaders to adjacent non-Devastated Regions under own or agreeing Faction's Control. Then roll for each Warband in a Devastated Region with no own Ally or Citadel, removed on 1-3.
   - Romans: may relocate Legions, Auxilia and Leader to adjacent non-Devastated Regions under Roman or agreeing Control. Then may move pieces from Supply Line Regions to Provincia. Each Legion or Auxilia outside Provincia: pay 1 Resource if the Region has a Roman Ally (2 if not, doubled if Devastated), or roll and lose on 1-3. One piece per Ally and per Fort stays free.
4. Harvest Phase (6.4), cap 45: Romans gain Resources equal to the victory score (Subdued + Dispersed + Allies). Arverni, Aedui, Belgae gain 2 per own Ally and Citadel. Aedui gain a further +4 (River Tolls).
5. Senate Phase (6.5):
   - Senate marker: Roman score below 10 shifts one row toward Uproar. 10 to 12 shifts toward Intrigue. Above 12 shifts toward Adulation. No shift down if any Legion is in the Fallen box. Shifting past an end flips to Firm. A shift opposite when Firm flips back to normal.
   - Legions: half the Fallen Legions (round down) stay out. The rest go to the Legions track, lowest rows first. Then all Legions on the Senate row or above go to Provincia. Roman player may not decline.
   - Auxilia: if the Roman Leader is in Provincia, place from Available: 3 Auxilia (Uproar), 4 (Intrigue), 5 (Adulation).
6. Spring Phase (6.6): place Successor Leaders where the Faction has a piece or Home symbol. Move remaining Fallen Legions to the Legions track. Remove Scouted markers and flip all Warbands and Auxilia Hidden. Remove Devastated markers. Remove Dispersed-Gathering markers, then flip Dispersed to Dispersed-Gathering (Razed stays). Mark all Factions Eligible. Play the next card.

54 BC special first Winter: Winter marker starts in Quarters. Skip the Victory Phase and the Germans Phase. At start of Harvest, Romans place 3 Legions from the Winter track into any Belgica Regions. At start of Senate Phase, set Senate to Intrigue (not Firm) and do not shift. In Spring, Arverni may place Vercingetorix from the Spring box in any Region (rules p.33).

Final Winter: if no one wins at the last Victory Phase, the game still ends. The Faction with the highest victory margin wins (2.4.1, 7.3). Ties: Non-players, then Romans, then Arverni, then Aedui (7.1). Rule 7.1 gives no tie position for Belgae, so a tie involving Belgae has no documented winner (rules decision needed). If any Non-player passes its victory check, all players lose (7.1).

## Non-player (bot) system

Applies to Roman and Gallic Factions only. Germans have fixed rules (6.2). Non-players use flowchart sheets plus rules 8.1 to 8.9. Full flowcharts are a separate sheet and are not in the rulebook. Not verified: any flowchart box not described in text.

General rules (8.1 to 8.4):
- Same costs and rules as players, except Limited Command becomes full Command plus Special Ability (8.1.1, 8.1.2).
- Event rule: decline Ineffective Events, Capabilities in the last year, and "No" Events (swords). Dual Use: Arverni and Belgae choose shaded, Romans and Aedui unshaded (8.1.1, 8.2.2).
- Event-driven actions follow the Faction flowchart. Non-players choose themselves for benefits. Select spaces to place or remove the most Legions, then Citadels, then Allies, then others (8.2.3, 8.3.1).
- Priorities: numbered priorities run in order, to the maximum extent. Lettered nested priorities break ties (8.1.3).
- Random choice: equal-chance die roll. For more than six candidates split into groups of six or fewer (8.3.4).
- Placing and removing: Leaders, then Legions, then Citadels or Forts, then Allies in Cities or at Remi, then other Allies, then Hidden before Revealed before Scouted enemy pieces. Target order: Belgae and Arverni target Romans, Aedui, then others, then Germans. Romans and Aedui target Arverni, Belgae, Germans, then others. End moves with at least 4 own Warbands or Auxilia with the Leader. Never voluntarily remove own pieces (8.4.1).
- Belgae and Arverni never transfer Resources and never agree to Supply Line, Retreat or Quarters in their Regions. They Harass Roman March and Seize. Aedui and Romans Harass Vercingetorix's March (8.4.2).
- Retreat when it saves the last Defending piece, when it lowers Loss rolls on Legions (Romans), or when they cannot guarantee half the Losses they will take (and have no Fort or Citadel). Gauls Retreating from Romans leave Hidden Warbands in place (8.4.3).
- During Frost, Non-players take no action that would push a player above or further beyond its victory threshold (8.4.4).

Belgae flowchart order (8.5):
1. Battle or March under threat: with Ambiorix or 4+ Warbands in a Region with a non-German enemy Ally, Citadel, Legion or 4+ pieces. Battle only where Losses inflicted exceed Losses taken and Ambiorix takes none. Ambush if it helps. Else Rampage before Battle, else Enlist. If no Battle, March threatened groups.
2. Pass or Event: if Belgae are first on the next card but not on the current card, roll 1-4 to Pass. Else execute the Event if allowed, not Ineffective and not "No".
3. Rally if it places an Ally, Citadel or 3+ Warbands, or adds Control. Allies first, then Warbands.
4. Raid if under 4 Resources and a 1-4 roll gains 2+. Take from players first.
5. March to add Control (needs 4+ Resources or a 5-6 roll).
- Quarters: leave Devastated Regions, join Leader to the largest group (8.5.6).

Aedui flowchart (8.6): check Pass (1-4 if first next card only) or Event. Then Battle only if a Loss on Leader, Ally, Citadel or Legion is certain, against Romans only if Aedui exceed their victory threshold. Rally if under 5 Warbands or good placement. Trade or Suborn (Trade if below 10 Resources, or Aedui plus Roman Resources under 15, or just Battled, and gains over 2 or is 2nd Eligible). Raid if under 4 Resources. March to spread. Non-player Aedui transfer 10 Resources to Romans when Roman Resources fall below 2 and Aedui hold over 20. They agree to Roman Supply Lines if the Roman is a Non-player, has a score under 10, or has 10 to 12 on a roll of 1-4 (8.6.6).

Arverni flowchart (8.7): Battle or March under threat (Vercingetorix or 10+ Warbands with a Roman or Aedui Ally, Citadel, Legion or 4+ pieces). Needs a forced Loss on a Legion or more Losses inflicted than suffered, with no Loss on Vercingetorix. With Caesar, Vercingetorix fights only with more than twice the mobile pieces. Ambush, else Devastate, else Entreat. Then Event (roll 1-4 to execute unless "Auto 1-4"). Rally if under 9 Warbands or placing 2 Allies and Citadels or 6 pieces. March to spread if under 6 Allies and Citadels or 6+ Available Warbands. Raid if under 4 Resources. March to mass. Quarters (8.7.7).

Romans flowchart (8.8): Battle or March under threat (Caesar or a Legion with enemy Ally, Citadel, Leader, Control or a threatened Loss). Battle only when Defender inflicts less than half the Losses of the Roman attack and none on Caesar. Besiege, then Scout. March if no Battle or an enemy sits at margin 0 or better while Caesar has 2+ Legions and 4+ Auxilia. Destination order uses victory margin, then die roll (1-2 Germans if 2+ Germanic Allies, 3-4 players first, else largest). Build before or after. Else Event. Else March if 8 or fewer Auxilia Available. Else Recruit (2+ Allies or 6+ pieces). Else Seize (Disperse all except Helvii, no Harassment). Else Pass. Quarters (8.8.7).

1-player victory (8.9): the player never wins before the final Winter. Must avoid any Non-player passing a victory check and hold the highest margin at the end. Easy option: win by exceeding the threshold at any Victory Phase. Difficult option: lose if any Non-player has a higher margin. Success level uses the margin gap (1-2, 3+).

## Unique mechanics

- Named Leaders tie Special Abilities to proximity (4.1.2).
- Tribal Allies (discs) replace bases and support tracks (Playbook p.27).
